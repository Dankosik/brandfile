# Name Check

The result is a dated record of risk with one of two conclusions: "low risk among what was checked", or "stop, see an attorney", never "free". A lawyer or patent attorney owns the conclusion.

1. List every form: each alphabet the name may appear in, transliterated both ways, the translation, abbreviations, lookalike letters. The person confirms the list.
2. Pick the working classes of goods and services, marked as a hypothesis for the attorney.
3. Search the word by text, sound, and meaning, and any logo by image.
4. Look beyond the registers: domains, company registers, marketplaces, social networks, the industry, well-known works and people.

For Russia, start with the free [Rospatent search platform](https://searchplatform.rospatent.gov.ru/trademarks). Check status and applications in the FIPS open registers; they may refuse automated access, so a person in Russia may need to look. Search the WIPO Global Brand Database only by hand, because its terms forbid automated queries. Applications appear with a delay, so an empty result says nothing about recent filings.

Record in `## Name`: the date, the forms searched, the classes, each database with its query and number of results, candidates with number and status, what could not be accessed, and the conclusion.

Stop and send to an attorney when:

- an identical or very similar mark is in the same or a neighboring class;
- the strong word matches despite different graphics, or Latin and Cyrillic forms sound alike;
- the translation matches the meaning of someone's strong mark;
- there is an earlier application, a famous brand, or a competitor really using the name;
- a register was unavailable, so the search is incomplete;
- a claim, a marketplace complaint, or a letter "from Rospatent" has arrived (verify it through official channels first);
- a marketplace launch or a large campaign is planned.

Since 1 March 2026, Russian consumer law requires Russian on signs and public information, except trademarks and company names, and the regulator reads that as registered marks only. A Latin name with no registration and no equal Russian form is at risk in consumer settings; B2B is unclear. Flag it for a lawyer.
