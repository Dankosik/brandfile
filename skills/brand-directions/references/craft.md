# Craft Notes

## Color

- Keep the brand's exact color in its role. Scale generators shift it, so use a mode that preserves it or build the scale around it.
- Give every meaning a second cue besides color, and make red and green differ in lightness as well as hue.
- Choose dark theme values on their own: inverted light values lose contrast and hue.
- Ask whether print or merchandise is real: each such surface needs its own reference value.
- Justify a color by its role, its contrast, and a fact in the brief. Rules of thumb such as 60-30-10, color wheel harmonies, and tables of what colors mean are not reasons.

## Type

- Choose in this order: what the text must do and where it lives, its languages and signs, the license, then one family. A second family needs a named reason: display, service text, code, or a base that fails at small sizes.
- Set real text from the brand, not a pangram. `glyphs` checks coverage, not drawing: ask the person to judge kerning, rhythm, and letterforms on the sheet.

## Marks

If they ask about a logo: the master is a vector drawn or cleaned by a person, and a wordmark is built from licensed type or drawn curves. Image models handle Cyrillic unreliably and leave the rights unclear.
