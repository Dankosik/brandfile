## 1. David Aaker: the identity model, the planning sequence, the point at which architecture is needed

### Takeaway

[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] In his own posts, Aaker starts from the desired associations and does not set a step-by-step analysis sequence. He has no threshold for architecture: instead of a threshold he asks three questions of each new offering. The three-part "planning model" from the book is not confirmed by his text. Only secondary sources show it.

### Cited Findings

**Identity model**

- [EXPERT OPINION] Core identity is described as the unchanging essence of the brand. Extended identity adds detail about what the brand should stand for. Example: Saturn. Four views of a brand: product, organization, person, symbol. In his words, organizational associations are harder to copy than product attributes. The source is not the book itself but a 1996 Berkeley press release with quotes from the author. Access: `digest`. — [UC Berkeley, 10.01.1996](https://newsarchive.berkeley.edu/news/media/releases/96legacy/releases.96/14299.html)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] The same release names a broad brand vision or identity, rather than a set of attributes, as the key step. It lists the book's topics in this order: identity, sub-brand systems, equity measurement, organizing the work. The release has no step-by-step process. In it Aaker also says that brands exist within a system of other brands: the portfolio theme is already in the 1996 book. Access: `digest`. — [UC Berkeley](https://newsarchive.berkeley.edu/news/media/releases/96legacy/releases.96/14299.html)
- [EXPERT OPINION] Aaker's own post: start with the set of desired associations, meaning what should come to mind when the brand is mentioned. The guide is six to twelve, of which two to four form the core. A single essence formula is optional and sometimes gets in the way. Brand strategy and business strategy are developed together. The post has no "customer, competitors, self-analysis" sequence. These are judgments with examples. Access: `digest`. — [Aaker, Prophet, 2011](https://prophet.com/2011/03/5-aakers-top-ten-brand-precepts/)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] The only number in the same post: across four studies with Bob Jacobson, the effect of brand building on stock returns was about 70% of the effect of a change in earnings. This is the author's self-report, and I did not read the studies themselves. Access: `digest`. — [Aaker, Prophet, 2011](https://prophet.com/2011/03/5-aakers-top-ten-brand-precepts/)
- [EXPERT OPINION] Secondary descriptions give 4 perspectives and 12 dimensions. One text from the NOVA repository criticizes the model because identity in it is conceived by the company and ignores the customer's perception. I did not see the three-part sequence "strategic analysis, identity system, implementation" in the author's text. Access: `description`. — [Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/aaker-brand-identity-model/); [Georgetown, diagram](https://diagrams.jrosborn.georgetown.domains/s/F2023/item/466); [NOVA](https://run.unl.pt/bitstreams/b9340151-d5fb-4964-b9af-e5b3bb9c1d29/download)

**Architecture**

- [EXPERT OPINION] Three questions for a new offering: will it strengthen the existing brands; will it strengthen an existing brand; is there a strong reason to create a new brand. A sub-brand gives some distance from the parent brand, an endorsed brand more, a separate brand the most. A separate step: keep the portfolio compact and do not create unneeded brands. The post is taken from the book Brand Portfolio Strategy. There is no data, and the examples serve as illustrations. Access: `digest`. — [Aaker, Prophet, 2012](https://prophet.com/2012/09/10-steps-to-a-successful-brand-portfolio-strategy/)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] This post has no threshold: not by number of products, not by revenue, not by portfolio size. Access: `digest`, so the absence was checked only by a question to the auxiliary model. — [Aaker, Prophet, 2012](https://prophet.com/2012/09/10-steps-to-a-successful-brand-portfolio-strategy/)
- [EXPERT OPINION] Aaker and Joachimsthaler 2000: the spectrum as a continuum of four basic strategies and nine sub-strategies. I did not read the article text: the old CMR address redirects to a Haas page, which returns 404, and SAGE shows only the beginning. Access: `description`. — California Management Review, 42(4), 8–23. [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/000812560004200401)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] The publisher describes Brand Portfolio Strategy as a book about decisions: when to stretch an existing brand and when to create a new brand, an endorsed brand, or a sub-brand. Access: `description`. — [Simon & Schuster](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Brand-Portfolio-Strategy/David-A-Aaker/9781982146528)

### Inferences

- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Aaker's questions give the report a more defensible trigger for architecture than "a second product with a different audience". The decision becomes an answer to a question about the new offering, not a consequence of counting products. This is the author's judgment, not a verified rule.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Aaker's advice not to compress a brand into a short phrase differs from the widespread practice of a "brand essence" and from our "phrase and paragraph" only in form: the discussion is about how many associations to keep, not about whether a short wording is needed for external text.

### Gaps

- Aaker 1996, Measuring Brand Equity Across Products and Markets (CMR): I made no separate query, and the article is not opened. Brand Equity Ten is not confirmed from this article.
- Building Strong Brands and Brand Leadership were not read. The sites davidaaker.com and aaker.com were not opened separately. A query restricted to the domains `prophet.com, aaker.com, davidaaker.com, cmr.berkeley.edu, hbr.org, simonandschuster.com` returned only Prophet and Simon & Schuster.
- The exact wording of the criteria for a new brand is not visible in the book.

## 2. Jean-Noël Kapferer: the prism and the definition of identity in his texts; replies to Ehrenberg-Bass

### Takeaway

[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] I could not get Kapferer's own text, so his definition of identity stays at the level of other people's descriptions. The queries I ran show no direct reply by Kapferer to the Ehrenberg-Bass school. What is new here is something else: the prism was in fact tested for factor structure, and the report's claim "no test was found" must be withdrawn.

### Cited Findings

- [EXPERT OPINION] Azoulay and Kapferer 2003: brand personality scales do not actually measure personality but merge several dimensions of identity. I could not legally get the full text: HAL-HEC is blocked by an anti-bot check, IDEAS reports that the file is missing, Springer redirects to a login, and I did not open the SlideShare copy. The level is unchanged. Access: `abstract`. — Journal of Brand Management, 11(2), 143–155. [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hal/journl/hal-00781544.html); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/palgrave.bm.2540162); [HAL](https://hal-hec.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00781544)
- [EXPERT OPINION] The prism: six facets (physique, personality, culture, relationship, reflection, self-image), a shift in management from image to identity. Only other people's descriptions. The dates of origin differ: 1986, 1992, and 1996. The formula "identity precedes image" is not confirmed as the author's own. Access: `description`. — [Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/kapferer-brand-identity-prism/); [Think Insights](https://thinkinsights.net/index.php/consulting/brand-identity-prism)
- [EMPIRICAL] Viot 2011: two studies. The first developed an identity inventory, and the second tested the prediction of extension success. The second-order structure of the prism is confirmed; identity helps predict the acceptance of extensions; it gives a fuller assessment of fit than single items. Sample sizes are not given on the page. This work links the prism to an outcome, but the outcome here is the consumer's acceptance of an extension, not brand growth. Access: `digest` of the abstract. — Journal of Product & Brand Management. [Emerald, DOI 10.1108/10610421111134941](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/10610421111134941/full/html)
- [EMPIRICAL] Barros, Martins, Barandas 2016: confirmatory factor analysis, 235 engineering students, identity measured inside and outside, the prism moderated by brand culture. The authors themselves write that a reliable and valid identity scale has not yet been created. A related chapter by the same author claims scales for higher education. The sample is students, and the context is one. Access: `description`. — International Journal of Innovation and Learning, 20(2), 214–231. [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/ijilea/v20y2016i2p214-231.html); [Inderscience](https://www.inderscience.com/filter.php?aid=77849); [chapter](https://ideas.repec.org/h/tkp/mklp15/2277.html)

**Null result: Kapferer's direct reply to the Ehrenberg-Bass school.** I ran three queries: `Kapferer response Byron Sharp "How Brands Grow" differentiation distinctiveness`; `Kapferer "New Strategic Brand Management" differentiation salience Ehrenberg Sharp identity precedes image definition brand identity`; and `Kapferer differentiation brands Ehrenberg-Bass Sharp` restricted to the domains `byronsharp.com, marketingscience.info, kapferer.com, hec.edu, theconversation.com`. His books, where such a reply may be, were not read, so the absence is not proven.

### Inferences

- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The prism test I found answers the question "does the measure break down into six facets", not "does a filled-in prism help a company". The report's recommendation to skip the prism in a small company may stand, but its basis cannot be the absence of any test.

## 3. Differentiation and distinctiveness: the texts of the sides, replies, independent tests

### Takeaway

[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The first pass missed the main point. A direct test of "differentiation versus distinctive assets" is still not visible, but independent works tested differentiation against real outcomes. Datta et al. 2017, on 290 brands and scanner data, found a weak negative link with sales-based equity, and a conditional one by category. Together with Stahl et al. 2012 and Mizik and Jacobson 2008, this adds up to a hypothesis: differentiation is linked to margin and to the effect of advertising more strongly than to volume. The report's wording "there is no direct test" must be replaced.

### Cited Findings

**The Ehrenberg-Bass side**

- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Correction to the bibliography. The article Differentiation or Salience came out in 1997, by Scriven, Barnard, and Ehrenberg, not Ehrenberg, Barnard, and Sharp 2000. Sharp is not listed among the authors, and I did not find a 2000 version. The spelling and order of the authors vary (Niel and Neil). Access: `digest` and `description`. — Journal of Advertising Research, 37(6), 7–14. [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/article/jar/differentiation-or-salience/en-gb/9076); [reference list](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/ref/10.1080/13527260600714700?scroll=top)
- [EXPERT OPINION] Content from the abstract: a critical review of differentiation and sustainable advantage. Salience is defined as the share of people who know the brand, consider it, or buy it, and usually goes together with distribution and sales. Advertising should make the brand noticeable to more people. A library record adds an argument: competing brands rarely differ much, because innovations that sell are quickly copied. I did not read the article. Access: `digest` of the abstract; `description`. — [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/article/jar/differentiation-or-salience/en-gb/9076); [BVB](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_205086369)
- [EMPIRICAL] Romaniuk, Sharp, Ehrenberg 2007: survey data, many categories, two countries. Perceived differences between competing brands are low, and buyers buy them anyway. The authors propose putting distinctiveness at the center. They do not themselves call differentiation useless: the role is more limited than the classics hold. I did not read the journal text: the copy on Academia returned 403. Pages 42–54 come from a search result for the lab's listing. Access: `description`. — Australasian Marketing Journal, 15(2). [EconBiz](https://econbiz.de/Record/evidence-concerning-the-importance-of-perceived-brand-differentiation-romaniuk-jenni/10003924370); [BrandKnew](https://www.brandknewmag.com/?p=21380)
- [EXPERT OPINION] Sharp, Answering critics: the critics are not named. Kapferer, Keller, Aaker, Kantar, and Millward Brown are not mentioned. The word differentiation does not appear in the text. He answers about loyalty, attitudes, and emotional decisions, citing McDonald and Ehrenberg 2003, Graham 2012, and a 2014 article in JBR. The page has no date, only "10 years ago". Access: `digest`. — [marketingscience.info](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/answering-critics)

**Replies**

- [VENDOR] Hollis (Millward Brown, 29.04.2011): meaningful differences are called a key driver of success, and a short-term advantage can leave a lasting sense of difference. Only the summary is visible on the page, and there is no data. Millward Brown sells the measurement of exactly this indicator. Access: `digest`. — [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/article/mbpoints/it-is-not-a-choice-brands-should-seek-differentiation-and-distinctiveness/en-gb/94550)
- [EXPERT OPINION] Ritson: separating the concepts is useless. Brands are not unique, but they differ relatively. Both salience and differentiation are needed. Access: `description`. — [Mini MBA](https://minimba.com/articles/in-the-battle-between-salience-and-differentiation-bothism-wins)
- [VENDOR] Kantar BrandZ: 1313 brands over three to four years, brands with a strong Difference score grow twice as often. On 40,000 brands there is a strong link between relative uniqueness and willingness to pay more. The vendor tests its own database on its own indicator. I did not check exactly which figure is on which of the pages. Access: `description`. — [Kantar, price](https://www.kantar.com/inspiration/brands/is-brand-differentiation-an-effective-way-to-reduce-customer-price-sensitivity); [Kantar, difference](https://www.kantar.com/Inspiration/Agile-market-research/Imbuing-brands-with-a-sense-of-difference)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Sharp reads the same BrandZ database the opposite way: an average of 18% agree that a brand is different. Both sides may be accurate at once: one speaks of the level of the indicator, the other of its link to growth. Access: first-pass notes. — [Sharp, blog](https://byronsharp.com/blog/files/millwardbrown_confirmation.html)

**Independent outcome tests (the first pass did not see them)**

- [EMPIRICAL] Datta, Ailawadi, van Heerde 2017. Panel: 290 brands, 25 categories of packaged goods, ten years of IRI scanner data and the BAV survey, 2002–2011. Relevance, esteem, and knowledge are fairly strongly positively linked to sales-based equity (the intercept in a choice or share model), and energized differentiation is weakly negatively linked. I did not see the coefficients, and the copy on CORE returned 403. The design is observational, causality is not shown, and it covers FMCG only. BAV belongs to an advertising group, and the authors are academics. Access: `digest` of the abstract. — Journal of Marketing, 81(3), [DOI 10.1509/jm.15.0340](https://doi.org/10.1509/jm.15.0340). - [EMPIRICAL] The same study, as retold by Bettencourt for AMA: differentiation overall has little effect on the sales response, but a noticeable effect in concentrated and experience categories (sensory properties and image decide the choice). The retelling contradicts itself in the diaper example. Access: `digest` of the retelling. — [AMA, 20.12.2017](https://www.ama.org/2017/12/20/are-you-managing-brand-equity-incorrectly)
- [EMPIRICAL] Stahl, Heitmann, Lehmann, Neslin 2012: the US auto market, ten years of data on acquisition, retention, and profitability together with BAV. Brand equity is double-edged: customer profitability is higher, but acquisition and retention are lower. Highly differentiated brands are probably aimed at narrower segments. The snippet comes from a working version: I did not confirm which pillars each direction applies to. The Columbia page returned 403, and MSI shows only an abstract without the pillars. Access: `description`. — Journal of Marketing, 76, 44–63, [DOI 10.1509/jm.10.0522](https://doi.org/10.1509/jm.10.0522). [MSI](https://www.msi.org/working-papers/the-impact-of-brand-equity-on-customer-acquisition-retention-and-profit-margin/); [Columbia](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/impact-brand-equity-customer-acquisition-retention-and-profit-margin/)

### Inferences

- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A hypothesis worth testing, not asserting: differentiation works on price and margin, while awareness, relevance, and esteem work on volume. Three signals support it. Stahl: profitability with lower acquisition. Datta: little sales response, but a larger advertising effect and a smaller discount effect. Mizik and Jacobson 2008: future accounting results. Kantar adds willingness to pay, but it is a vendor. All the works except Kantar rest on one instrument, BAV.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The report's wording is correct for data on distinctiveness: Datta does not measure assets. But the weakness of differentiation as a predictor of sales now has independent confirmation on FMCG, so it is no longer the position of one institute. The conclusion is conditional by category, and there is no B2B in it.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Salience in Scriven and coauthors includes purchase. The link between such salience and sales is partly built into the definition, so it is not an independent test.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The link between survey indicators and past use (Romaniuk et al. 2012 in the report) also applies to the positive links of relevance and esteem with sales in Datta. Whether the panel model accounts for this cannot be checked without the text.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] For the brand book: the weight of the reason to choose depends on category and goal. In a concentrated or experience category, and with the goal of holding price, the reason to choose matters more. In a broad category with many competitors, and with the goal of widening the base, salience and identifiers matter more.

### Gaps

- The Datta and Stahl coefficients are not visible. The text of Romaniuk, Sharp, Ehrenberg 2007 is not read.
- The material `marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/how-b2b-brands-grow` was not opened, and the B2B question is key for these conclusions.
- The article by Edeling and Fischer and other meta-analyses of brand equity were not checked.

## 5. Brand personality meta-analyses and the work after Avis et al. 2014

### Takeaway

[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Meta-analyses exist, and their scale has grown to 95 articles and 1441 effects, but they sum up correlations and do not settle the dispute over validity: the 2025 update does not mention Avis, Azoulay, or Kapferer at all. The strongest antecedent there is the respondent's own personality (r = 0.462), which by itself calls for a careful reading.

### Cited Findings

- [EMPIRICAL] Eisend, Stokburger-Sauer 2013: a meta-analysis, 76 independent samples from 56 works. Antecedents: hedonic benefits in messages, branding actions, country of origin, consumer personality. Consequences: attitude toward the brand, image, commitment, purchase intention. Sincerity and competence give the largest effects, excitement and ruggedness the smallest. Effects are stronger for mature brands. The page has no effect values. Access: `digest` of the abstract. — Marketing Letters, 24(3), 205–216, [DOI 10.1007/s11002-013-9232-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11002-013-9232-7).
