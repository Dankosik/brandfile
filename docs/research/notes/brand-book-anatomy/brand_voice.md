## 1. Definitions: how voice differs from tone, whether this is agreed, and where it came from

### Takeaway
[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The formula "voice stays constant, tone changes" is a common practical convention in product guides, not an agreed definition. British government guides and academic papers often speak of one object, tone of voice, and no open source gives data in favor of the split.

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The skills need their own working definition, because there is no agreed one to borrow. A practical option: voice is the rules that always apply and can be checked on any text. Tone is a set of shifts to those rules by situation, defined in advance. Such a definition is valuable because it is checkable, not because it is "correct".
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A skill that reads someone else's guide must not treat the words voice and tone as unambiguous: "how we say it" is called voice at Microsoft and tone at Intuit.

### Gaps
- The origin of the voice/tone pair is not confirmed. I could not verify the launch date of voiceandtone.com or its authorship (from memory: Kate Kiefer Lee, around 2011), because search was blocked.
- I did not check which meaning of tone literary scholars introduced (Moran refers to the literary use, with no source).

## 2. Evidence: how style affects trust, liking, satisfaction, and purchase intent

### Takeaway
[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Direct effects exist, but almost all are conditional. An informal or "human" voice works with a familiar brand, low risk, and a hedonic goal. With an unfamiliar brand, high involvement, and after a service failure, it can hurt. The results most usable in writing concern narrow features: "I" instead of "we", and concreteness. Effect sizes are visible in few places. I found no direct replications and no meta-analysis, and the base of marketing replications looks worrying.

Effects by context:
- [EMPIRICAL] Complaint and service. "I" beats "we". In one case, the possessive "your" in an apology for a defect lowered satisfaction and purchase intent, because it shifted blame onto the customer (Packard et al., [MIT SMR](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/how-should-companies-talk-to-customers-online)). Concreteness raises satisfaction (Packard, Berger). Emoji lower perceived competence for customers with an exchange orientation, and a service failure makes this norm more salient (Li et al.). Negative comments cancel the plus of a human voice (Barcelos et al.).
- [EMPIRICAL] Humor in reply to a complaint. Works only with caveats and was measured on observers (Béal, Grégoire). Follow-up: Béal, Grégoire, Carrillat 2023, Journal of Interactive Marketing: humor is stronger against a rude complaint, and an apology is stronger against a polite one. Access: snippet. — [EconBiz](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/let-s-laugh-about-it-using-humor-to-address-complainers-online-incivility/10014228864)
- [EMPIRICAL] Low trust and high risk. In Moran, humor in insurance lowered trust by 0.3 and did not change willingness to recommend, but in home security it raised trust and recommendation by 0.4. The bank's conversational but serious tone beat the dry one: friendliness +0.7, trust +0.3, recommendation +0.4. Hospital: the warm tone is friendlier (+0.5), trust is higher at the border of significance (p = 0.05). Regression: trust explains 52% of the variation in willingness to recommend, friendliness adds about 8%. The "±" values are not defined in the article. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-voice-users/)
- [EMPIRICAL] Person versus company. Packard et al.: an agent who says "I" is seen as acting for the customer, while "we" blurs responsibility. Sela et al.: "we" on behalf of a brand is appropriate only with closeness.
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] A company claim without data: Intuit writes that light-hearted AI remarks tested poorly with customers. There is no sample, method, or dates. — [Intuit: Tone](https://contentdesign.intuit.com/ai/voice-tone/tone/)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Simplicity: GOV.UK justifies mandatory plain English with literacy data (Literacy Trust: from 1 in 8 to 1 in 4 adults across the parts of the UK) and a study in which 80% preferred clear English in legal documents. I did not open the studies themselves. — [GOV.UK: clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)

Contradictions:
- [EMPIRICAL] The benefit of "you" is disputed: Packard et al. find no benefit and find harm when "you" is the subject, while Cruz et al. find a benefit under low collectivism. The contexts differ: a support reply and a brand post.
- [EMPIRICAL] The benefit of humor is disputed even within one study: in Moran it lost on trust in insurance and won in home security. With one pair of texts per industry, this cannot be separated from the quality of the specific text.
- [EMPIRICAL] T/V: the positive effects of informal address sit next to a null result in the museum study.

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The most stable finding in the available data: readers judge how appropriate a voice is relative to the relationship and the risk, not warmth as such. Moderators recur in independent studies: familiarity (Gretry, Sela, Packard Study 4), risk and involvement (Barcelos, Moran, partly Sela), relationship norm (Li).
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The best candidates for checkable rules are narrow: who is the subject (I, we, you), whether there is a concrete object instead of a pronoun or a general word, whether there is an emoji or a joke in a negative situation. "Friendliness" as a goal is poorly supported: in Moran it is linked to willingness to recommend more weakly than trust is.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The revenue growth in Packard et al. cannot be carried over as a promise: it is a correlation in one company's data, and the summaries differ (above 5% and up to 7%). The scale of effects is small, so a brand book should honestly promise consistency and lower risk, not sales growth.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Most experiments were run on online panels and scenarios in English. External validity for Russian B2B is not established. This is partly why a meta-analysis is hard: CHV itself is operationalized with different sets of features.

### Gaps
- Error messages in interfaces: I did not read any controlled studies. Search was blocked. Candidates from memory, unchecked and not used: Tzeng (2004) and Akgun et al. (2010) on apologetic messages.
- Crolic et al. (2022), Blame the Bot, Journal of Marketing, from memory about anthropomorphic chatbots and angry customers: I could not read it, the RePEc page returned 404, and search was blocked. I do not use it in the conclusions.
- Effect sizes (d, η²) for Packard, Berger, Sela, Gretry, Barcelos, Li, Béal are unavailable without full texts.
- I found no meta-analysis of CHV or brand style with a single query. This is weak evidence of absence.
- The snippet summary "people were 19% more satisfied and 15% more willing to buy" for Packard et al. appears only on a secondary site, [Science Says](https://app.sciencesays.com/p/say-i-not-we-to-customers), and is not confirmed by the primary source. Do not use without the full text.

## 3. Audit of public guides

### Takeaway
[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] 11 guides were opened. The structure repeats: 3–7 adjectives or principles, tone by situation or channel, examples, rules for words and mechanics, an inclusive-language section. Numeric thresholds exist in only two (GOV.UK, Kontur), and I saw automatic text checking only at PostHog. Mechanics and word lists can be checked by machine. Adjectives can be checked only by a human or a judging model.

## 4. How voice is derived: practitioners' methods, their inputs, and validation

### Takeaway
[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] I found no validated methods. The most worked-out public check is at NN/g, and it confirms only that readers notice a difference in tone, not that the four axes are right. Methods differ in their inputs: product principles, a corpus of "good" texts, the audience's fears, or a character.

### Cited Findings
- [EXPERT OPINION] Podmajersky, voice chart: a table with a column for each product principle and six rows (concepts, vocabulary, verbosity, grammar, punctuation, register); designed to fit on one page. Uses: design a voice, train new writers, settle a dispute when there is no test. Input: product principles. Support: her personal experience in several companies; no data. She calls the method an adaptation of familiar criteria tables. Access: 2019 interview. The book itself was not read (O'Reilly returned 403). — [UXpod](https://uxpod.com/episodes/ux-writing-an-interview-with-torrey-podmajersky.html)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] VTEX confirms the six rows and shows a filled-in example, citing O'Reilly, 2019. — [VTEX](https://uxwriting.vtex.com/docs/principles/voice-chart/)
- An agency pointer, not evidence: Boldare adds inputs such as brand values, archetype, and workshops; it gives no validation. — [Boldare](https://www.boldare.com/blog/voice-chart-in-ux-writing)
- [EXPERT OPINION] Moran, four axes: formal/informal, serious/funny, respectful/irreverent, matter-of-fact/enthusiastic. Practical steps: choose a profile without extremes, refine it with tone words and anti-words, test with users through product reaction or Likert scales. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/)
- [EMPIRICAL] What Moran rests on. Deriving the axes: from a long list of literary tone words (from the pages of English courses for students), the author selected 37 applicable to websites, then merged close ones and removed words with no antonym or too narrow. The article has no factor analysis and no number of sites analyzed. Check: 4 pairs of texts from fictional sites, an online survey of 50 Americans; they rated friendliness and formality on a 5-point scale; the difference within pairs is significant at p < 0.05, on average 0.5–1 point. These two characteristics were tested, not all four axes as a structure. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/)
- [EMPIRICAL] What Moran's second article rests on: 100 American adults, text samples, fictional organizations, one pair per industry; a qualitative phase with no N stated; the tests for pairwise comparisons are not named, and corrections for multiple comparisons are not mentioned. The author herself notes the subjectivity of tone ratings and the artificial nature of the samples. The article was revised 2024-01-30; the retelling notes no new data. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-voice-users/)
- [EMPIRICAL] Corpus method. Henriksen et al. (2004): companies chose "good" texts with the right tone (Nordea: 198,996 words in 92 texts; Bang & Olufsen: 87,739 words in 25 texts), the authors manually picked value-laden words from frequency lists, compared pronouns with the BNC, and analyzed modality and who does what in a sentence. The authors propose implementing tone through word lists and frequency thresholds in a controlled-language tool. The parts I read contain no reader test. Tone rules tend to encourage features rather than ban them, unlike readability rules. — [CST](https://www.cst.dk/vid/public/rapport-4-tone-of-voice.pdf)
- [EMPIRICAL, descriptive] Delin (2005) proposes similar tools on HSBC and Orange; hypotheses are stated but not tested in the abstract. Access: snippet. — [Equinox](https://journal.equinoxpub.com/JALPP/article/view/13135)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Russian case: РСХБ.Цифра (2020) first wrote an editorial policy, then derived rules from farmers' fears, making a business partner the character. There were usability tests, no numbers. — [Habr, РСХБ.Цифра](https://habr.com/ru/post/531456)
- [EXPERT OPINION] The Growbots case in translation: research, team discussion, a character, a matrix of two axes (professionalism and energy). No metrics. — [Habr, translation by Toczyska, 2019](https://habr.com/ru/post/446052)
- [VENDOR] Click.ru: a description of the brand's "DNA", portraits, an audit of current communications, a word map after NN/g; it says outright that there are no exact metrics. — [Habr, Click.ru, 2022](https://habr.com/post/702534). Sber: study the audience and values, describe the brand as a character, test, set up channels. — [Sber Developers](https://developers.sber.ru/help/salutespeech/tone-of-voice)

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The minimal set of inputs shared by the methods: who the reader is and what they fear, which product decisions need expressing, a few of the company's own texts that it considers successful. A workshop is a form, not an input.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] For an agent skill, the corpus path fits best: Henriksen et al. show that tone can be broken into measurable features, though the effect on readers is not tested. The skill can collect features from the texts itself, and a human confirms the choice.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The NN/g axes work as conversational language for choosing a profile, but citing them as a proven model is inaccurate. In a brand book it is better to write: "we use the axes as a convenient convention".

### Gaps
- I found no comparisons of methods against each other, for example audit versus workshop.
- The books by Fenton, Kiefer Lee; Richards; Halvorson, Rach; Yifrah; Handley; Simmons and Podmajersky's book itself were not read, and claims from them are not used. The second edition of Podmajersky (2025) mentions AI only per a snippet.
- How exactly companies run a content audit for voice was not checked against primary sources.

## 5. The minimal voice section for a small company

### Takeaway
[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The whole section is an inference, not a found fact. Minimum: one page of rules and 8–10 pairs of examples from the company's own texts. No source tested that this volume is enough.

### Cited Findings
Grounds for the inference:
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] A constant voice and a changing tone are explicitly stated in 7 of 11 guides (Mailchimp, Microsoft, Shopify, Atlassian, NHS, Intuit, PostHog); Monzo is close in substance. A tone map by situation, channel, or role exists in 7 of 11 (Shopify, Intuit, Monzo, Atlassian, NHS, PostHog, Kontur). Before/after or do/don't pairs exist in 6 of 11 (Microsoft, Kontur, PostHog, Shopify, Intuit, Google). I saw inclusivity rules in 5 of 11 (Mailchimp, GOV.UK, Monzo, Microsoft, Intuit). Numeric thresholds in 2 of 11 (GOV.UK, Kontur). — see the audit in section 3.
- [EXPERT OPINION] Podmajersky: a chart should fit on one page, and a style guide is often tiring. — [UXpod](https://uxpod.com/episodes/ux-writing-an-interview-with-torrey-podmajersky.html)
- [EMPIRICAL] Narrow features are empirically supported: who speaks and in which person (Packard, Sela), concreteness (Packard, Berger), appropriateness of informality by familiarity and risk (Gretry, Barcelos). Links in section 2.

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Contents of the minimum, in priority order:
  1. Who speaks: roles and persons. System, support, company. Basis: Packard, Kontur.
  2. Address: "вы" [you, formal] or "ты" [you, informal], register, name or not. Basis: a feature of Russian and the T/V studies.
  3. Three principles in the form "X, not Y", each with one check. Basis: Shopify, GOV.UK.
  4. A tone map for 4–6 situations: routine action, success, user error, our error or complaint, money and risk, news. For each: what to strengthen, what is banned. Basis: Shopify, Monzo, Atlassian; support from Li, Barcelos.
  5. 10–15 hard rules: sentence length, banned words and replacements, punctuation, emoji. Add one or two lines on inclusivity here: how to write without specifying gender, how to name people. A full section is not found in every guide (I saw it in 5 of 11).
  6. 8–10 before/after pairs from the company's own texts. Basis: Microsoft, Kontur.
  7. A glossary of 10–20 terms. Basis: Kontur.
  8. How we check: read aloud, a linter or a model, once a quarter one small test on customers. Basis: Monzo, Google, NN/g, PostHog.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] What can be postponed: archetypes, a mascot, a long history of the voice, spectrums with scores. They have no empirical support in the sources read, and they cannot be checked automatically.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] An illustration of the form on a fictional delivery-tracking service, my own example:
  - Speaker: the system impersonally; support in "я" [I] with a name; the company in "мы" [we] only about its own decisions.
  - Address: "вы" [you, formal], lowercase letter.
  - Principle: "Конкретно, а не оптимистично" [Concrete, not optimistic]. Check: every promise has a number, a deadline, or an action.
  - Tone for our error: no jokes or emoji; first what happened, then what we are doing, then the deadline.
  - Pair: before «Возникла ошибка при обработке запроса» [An error occurred while processing the request]. After «Не сохранили накладную: нет адреса. Укажите адрес и сохраните снова» [We could not save the waybill: no address. Enter the address and save again].
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] An honest wording for the report: the minimum rests on repetition across guides and on separate experiments about features, not on a test of the set itself.

### Gaps
- There is no data on how many examples people need to apply a guide uniformly. For models there is indirect data (section 8).
- The sentence-length threshold for Russian has no basis other than Kontur's convention.

## 6. Why guides do not work: shared and indistinguishable voices

### Takeaway
[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] There is almost no direct evidence, and the claim about shared voices rests on opinions. There are indirect signs: a gap between the rule and real texts in one corpus report, different definitions of one feature, and matching wording across public guides.

### Cited Findings
- [EXPERT OPINION] Claire Lynch (Ragan, 2013): such guides teach professionals nothing new, and other employees ignore them or read them through their own idea of corporate tone. There is no data, and the page is partly behind a paywall. — [Ragan](https://www.ragan.com/why-corporate-tone-of-voice-guidelines-fail/)
- [EXPERT OPINION] Efimov (Habr, personal blog): a uniform friendly-businesslike style lowers the cost of switching to another brand. In briefs, tone is usually given one sentence. No measurements. — [Habr](https://habr.com/ru/articles/944950/)
- [VENDOR] Click.ru lists typical mistakes: copying someone else's voice, changing it sharply, joking where a problem needs solving, having no guide. No data. — [Habr](https://habr.com/post/702534)
- [EMPIRICAL] Henriksen et al. (2004): companies recommend we/us/our, but the authors see no significant difference from the general BNC corpus; the second person is more frequent. Value-laden words are mostly low-frequency. Sample: 2 companies, English texts by Danish firms. — [CST](https://www.cst.dk/vid/public/rapport-4-tone-of-voice.pdf)
- [EMPIRICAL] Liebrecht et al. (2021): different studies define the same concept of human voice with different features. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbrese:v:132:y:2021:i:c:p:124-135)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Overlaps: Mailchimp and Intuit's AI section use the same words plainspoken and genuine ([Mailchimp](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/), [Intuit AI](https://contentdesign.intuit.com/ai/voice-tone/)); at the same time Mailchimp is listed as a brand at Intuit ([home](https://contentdesign.intuit.com/)). Almost all guides ask for clarity and humanity, including Microsoft, PostHog, GOV.UK, NHS.
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] The guides themselves warn about empty adjectives: Monzo about evaluative words and clichés, NN/g about anti-words, PostHog about filler words. — [Monzo](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/); [PostHog](https://posthog.com/handbook/brand/tone)
- [EMPIRICAL] Opoku, Abratt, Pitt (2006), Journal of Brand Management 14(1–2): a brand personality dictionary based on Aaker applied to the websites of 11 business schools. This is a method for measuring projected personality, not a test of how alike voices are. Access: snippet. — [Publication abstract](https://faculty.kfupm.edu.sa/MGM/opoku/Publication_Abstracts/Journal_of_Brand_Management.pdf)
- [EMPIRICAL] Case descriptions from snippets: a Serbian study of editors revising texts under corporate guides ([Godišnjak](https://godisnjak.ff.uns.ac.rs/index.php/gff/article/download/2257/2127), could not be opened); the bank LLB builds its recommendations around four parameters with training ([LLB](https://voices.llb.li/en/?p=1303)).

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] In a sample of 11 guides, adjectives distinguish companies poorly. This is an observation, not a test on readers. Rules, roles, and bans differ noticeably more: Microsoft asks for contractions, GOV.UK limits them, Monzo allows jokes, Intuit avoids them in its AI section.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A simple distinguishability test for a skill: remove the name and ask whether this voice would suit a direct competitor. If yes, the principle does not work. The test itself is not validated.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Causes of failure that can be justified: uncheckability (adjectives), a gap from real texts (Henriksen), no examples and no process (Mailchimp on the voice page and Atlassian on the voice page without pairs; applying them there requires other sections). The cause "no difference from competitors" remains a hypothesis.

### Gaps
- I found no studies in which readers told brands apart by text style alone; search was limited.
- The literature on distinctiveness versus brand differentiation (Romaniuk, Sharp) and on brand personality (Aaker) was not checked. The pointers [Instrument](https://instrument.com/latest/the-paradox-of-guidelines) and [Sostav](https://www.sostav.ru/blogs/289099/96546) are agency blogs; I did not read them and do not use them as evidence.

## 7. Russian: address, gender, errors, инфостиль [infostyle], public guides, experiments

### Takeaway
[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] I found no controlled Russian experiments on how style affects trust, but the search was incomplete. There is linguistics on the variability of the «ты/вы» [informal/formal "you"] norms, and there are preference surveys. The only detailed public Russian guide I found is Kontur. Many rules of English guides do not carry over directly: the English you is neutral, while in Russian the choice of «ты» or «вы» sets distance at once.

### Cited Findings

Address:
- [EMPIRICAL, linguistics] Шмелёв (2016), «Ты и вы в русском речевом этикете: вариативность норм» [Ty and vy in Russian speech etiquette: variability of norms] (RSUH edition): norms differ by social group and region, and speakers disagree even in simple cases; the descriptions by Zholkovsky and Melchuk and by Wierzbicka have opposite logic about which form counts as the default. Verb forms themselves are perceived as a sign of «ты» or «вы». Access: full text, in parts. — [РГГУ](https://history.rsuh.ru/jour/article/viewFile/214/215)
- [EMPIRICAL, linguistics] Евсеева (2012), Вестник КемГУ no. 4 (52), vol. 3 [Kemerovo State University Bulletin]: a review of online norms. A forum vote on «ты» with a stranger online gave roughly equal numbers of votes. This is an observation of a discussion, not an experiment. Access: full text, in parts. — [PDF](https://www.sibscript.ru/jour/article/download/3704/3253)
- [EMPIRICAL, survey] Жукова (around 2012): a survey of Muscovites aged 20–50 in 2010–2011, sample size not stated in the parts I viewed. With clients: «Вы» forms 34%, full name 30%, first name and patronymic 27%. The data are old and self-reported. Access: full text, in parts. — [НИУ ВШЭ](https://pravo.hse.ru/data/2012/12/04/1301970409/%D0%9D%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%8B%D0%B5%20%D1%82%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B4%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%86%D0%B8%D0%B8%20%D0%B2%20%D1%84%D1%83%D0%BD%D0%BA%D1%86%D0%B8%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B8%20%D0%BE%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%89%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B9%20%D0%B2%20%D0%B4%D0%B5%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%BC%20%D0%BE%D0%B1%D1%89%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B8%20%D0%96%D1%83%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0%20%D0%A2%D0%A1_red.pdf)

Gender:

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Rules from English guides that cannot be carried over directly:

| English rule | Source | What it is in Russian | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Address as you | GOV.UK, PostHog, Intuit | you is neutral, while the choice of «ты» or «вы» sets distance and runs through the verb and the imperative | A separate decision in the brand book, a check that forms are consistent |
| Contractions as a sign of warmth | Microsoft, PostHog; GOV.UK limits them | There are no contractions | Other signals of informality: «ты», particles, word order |
| Singular they and he/she | Microsoft, Mailchimp, GOV.UK | No analog; the past tense shows gender | Impersonal forms, present tense, describing the result, «вы» |
| «Вы» removes gender | inference, norm not checked against a reference book | The verb and the short adjective in the plural, but full adjectives and nouns show gender | The linter must check such constructions too |
| Active voice always | GOV.UK, Monzo, Kontur in its editorial policy | In an interface an impersonal participle is normal, and Kontur itself writes this way for the robot | The rule is conditional on the speaker |
| «Я» instead of «мы» | Packard et al. | Not tested in Russian. The verb shows person without a pronoun, and shows the author's gender. A pronoun count will miss such cases | Count verb morphology, not words |
| Oxford comma, dashes without spaces, sentence case | Microsoft | Different punctuation and typography; sentence case is the norm anyway | Its own rules: quotation marks, dashes, «ё», non-breaking spaces |
| Ban on please | GOV.UK, Google | VTEX, on the contrary, asks for please and thank you. There is no data on the Russian «пожалуйста» [please] | Decide it as a choice of voice, not as a universal rule |
| Sentence-length threshold | GOV.UK 25 words | Kontur has 12–15. Comparability not checked | Calibrate on your own corpus |
| Substituting names and numbers | English templates | Cases, agreement with numerals, declension of brands; Kontur separately covers the declension of its own names | Check templates, not only text |

- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A practice for the brand book: by default «вы» in lowercase. The support is weak: the stated preferences in Anketolog, Kontur, Sber. «Ты» is a deliberate decision after a test on your own audience. Basis: the English T/V results and the studies on familiarity point to conditionality, but they are not tested for Russian.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Infostyle should be treated as basic clarity, not as a voice. If everyone uses it, it raises comprehensibility but gives no distinctiveness. Its stop words are an editor's heuristics, and applying them as a hard ban without a human is not advisable. Part of its logic agrees indirectly with the data on concreteness (Packard, Berger), but in English and in service, not as a test of the method itself.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The NN/g formula came into Russian practice through secondary texts (Sber, Click.ru) as a ready-made model. Its status from section 4 needs to be explained here as well.

## 8. Voice for AI: public examples and studies

### Takeaway
[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The best public example I found is Intuit's AI section, but its prompt and rubric did not open. Studies show that models follow checkable instructions well, partly pick up formal styles from examples, and drift toward a shared average tone. I found no comparisons of "adjectives versus rules" for brands. There is no data in Russian.

### Cited Findings
Public examples:
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Intuit: one AI voice for all products, the image of a guide, two attributes (plainspoken, genuine), a neutral-professional tone by default, Mailchimp as an exception. It bans hyperbole, upselling, manipulation, empty metaphors, overconfidence, verbosity. It says prompts are the main tool for tuning. No date, © 2026. — [Voice & tone](https://contentdesign.intuit.com/ai/voice-tone/); [How to sound like Intuit](https://contentdesign.intuit.com/ai/voice-tone/how-to-get-ai-to-sound-like-intuit/)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Intuit, prompting: a universal voice block is inserted into the system prompt and does not cover format and vocabulary; it advises input-output pairs, context, and negative instructions. The prompt and rubric are marked as experimental. Both spreadsheets at the links returned 401, and Intuit also notes that Markdown files may go stale. — [Prompting](https://contentdesign.intuit.com/ai/using-ai/prompting/)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Intuit, evaluation: people review and label batches of responses in a spreadsheet against quality standards and edit the prompts. The page has no criteria, scales, or thresholds. — [Evaluation](https://contentdesign.intuit.com/ai/using-ai/evaluation/)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] PostHog: the Vale linter checks pull requests, the Inkeep agent uses the guide as context, skills for agents are announced as the next step. No quality numbers. — [PostHog docs style guide](https://posthog.com/handbook/docs-and-wizard/docs-style-guide.md)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Atlassian: a snippet mentions team standards for AI content, and the open voice page does not link to them. — [Atlassian Content](https://atlassian.design/foundations/content)
- [VENDOR] Commercial settings: Frase applies rules to drafts, Zendesk has a tone setting for its AI agent. Access: snippets, no independent check. — [Frase](https://help.frase.io/article/175-intro-to-content-governance); [Zendesk review by eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/zendesk-ai-tone-and-brand-voice-settings)
- Pointers without the status of evidence: an agency blog claiming that adjectives do not transfer to a model ([Sostav](https://www.sostav.ru/blogs/289099/96546)); one author's dataset with a machine voice specification ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/thehonestape/brand-voice-spec)); third-party skill catalogs. Not read.

Studies:
- [EMPIRICAL] Wang et al. (2025), Findings of EMNLP 2025, preprint arXiv:2509.14543: about 400 ordinary authors in 4 datasets (Enron, blogs, the news set CCAT50, Reddit), 6 models, more than 40,000 generations per model, 5 examples versus zero. Top-5 attribution with examples: news 87–93%, emails 56–62%, blogs 38–44%, Reddit 26–36%. Without examples, much worse on the informal datasets. Examples bring output closer to the author, but worse than humans. Going from 2 to 10 examples gives almost nothing. Selecting examples by topic closeness lowered attribution in emails, Reddit, and blogs. Generations slide toward a shared average tone. Limits: English only, no large human evaluation, within one genre, authors rather than brands. Access: page. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14543v1)
- [EMPIRICAL] Reinhart et al. (2025), PNAS 122: parallel corpora (8,290 and 9,615 texts), 66 Biber features, GPT-4o and Llama 3 variants. Instruction-tuned models use nominalizations 1.5–2 times more often than humans (GPT-4o 2.1 times, d = 1.23), participial phrases 2–5 times more often (GPT-4o 5.3 times, d = 1.38), and GPT-4o uses agentless passives about half as often. A random forest tells 7 sources apart with 66% accuracy against a chance level of 14%. The prompt asked to continue in the same style, and the differences persisted, though there is no separate test of such a prompt. English. Access: page. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v2)
- [EMPIRICAL] Zhou et al. (2023), IFEval, arXiv:2311.07911: 25 types of checkable instructions (banned words, keyword frequency, number of words and paragraphs), about 500 prompts. Strict accuracy: GPT-4 76.89% by prompt and 83.57% by instruction, PaLM 2 Small 43.07% and 55.76%. The responses were collected in August and November 2023, and the models are different now. Access: full text, in parts. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07911)
- [EMPIRICAL] Ramu et al. (2025), StyleAdaptedLM, preprint arXiv:2507.18294, peer review not confirmed: LoRA adapters trained on style texts, merged into an instruction-following model. The authors claim better style consistency without losing instruction following, and humans confirmed the uptake of brand conventions. I did not see the numbers. This is fine-tuning, not a prompt. Access: abstract. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18294)

Contradictions:
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT and EMPIRICAL] Intuit calls input-output pairs very effective; Wang et al. confirm a gain over zero but record saturation and weakness on informal style. These are different tasks: a brand and a personal author.

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] For a generation skill, a layered specification is reasonable: first checkable constraints, then 3–5 examples across different situations, then a separate deterministic checker. The basis is indirect: IFEval for the first, Wang et al. for the second, the non-zero share of non-compliance in IFEval for the third.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Examples are best chosen by type of situation, not by topic. This follows indirectly from the drop in attribution in Wang et al., but it was tested only on authors and in English.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The checker should watch for the known shift in models: nominalizations and participial phrases. This overlaps with the Russian rules of Kontur and infostyle, but Reinhart's measurements were made in English.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A model will hold formal voices more easily than playful ones: news and emails are reproduced better than blogs and forums. In practice this means a playful brand needs a stricter check and a human.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A judging model must be calibrated on human-labeled examples: neither Intuit nor PostHog has published thresholds.

### Gaps
- No comparison of different ways to specify a brand voice (adjectives, rules, examples) on one task was found. No exact numeric expectations of a skill can be given.
- I did not look for Russian-language studies of how LLMs hold style: limit.
- Prohibitive instructions: Intuit recommends them, but I did not check studies of how models follow bans in long texts; IFEval covers only word bans.
- Intuit's universal prompt and rubric: the [prompt spreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1FOy6SohDc8xP8E_YvEtcLal8mAHfar8Hb6yJ9QYbJGQ/edit?usp=sharing) and the [rubric spreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1AIknLM9O_R4Vr4ntOgxZx632Pt0GsfGgRbBKrFe1pPY/edit?gid=0#gid=0) returned 401; I did not try to sign in.
