## Medium: PDF, website, document or Notion, Figma file, repository, physical binder

### Takeaway
I found no direct evidence that any brand book medium is used more or gives more consistency: there are no comparative studies in the sources available to me. There is indirect data on the differences between web and PDF, surveys on the role of templates and managers' example, and one strong precedent: in March 2026 GOV.UK removed the PDF and kept the website.

### Cited Findings

**Direct data on how guidelines are applied**
- [EMPIRICAL] Survey of employees of 20 large Dutch organizations: templates and access to current rules for all employees are common and considered important, but how often the measures are applied, how important they are seen to be, and how effective they are barely line up; the underused and decisive factor turned out to be managers' personal example. The abstract does not give the number of respondents, and there is no comparison of media. (abstract) — [van den Bosch, de Jong, Elving 2004, Public Relations Review 30(2)](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/managing-corporate-visual-identity-use-and-effects-of-organizatio/)
- [EMPIRICAL] The same authors, employees of 20 organizations, structural modeling: tools and support, socialization, and knowledge of the strategy strongly affect consistency. Consistency was measured by employees' perception, not by a visual audit. (abstract) — [van den Bosch, Elving, de Jong 2006, European Journal of Marketing 40(7/8)](https://research.hanze.nl/nl/publications/the-impact-of-organisational-characteristics-on-corporate-visual-/)
- [EMPIRICAL, weak] House of Lords internal memo, 2010: staff believe that rules and templates gave the materials authority, but some staff do not go to the central team. (by description, file unavailable: DNS error) — [House of Lords Management Board paper](https://webtest.parliament.uk/globalassets/documents/hl-managementboardpapers/9th-meeting/mb-2010-10-hl-visualidentity.pdf)

**PDF versus web**
- [TRANSFER] NN/g: a long PDF on screen is disorienting, scans poorly, is harder to update, and an out-of-date version undermines trust. Acceptable cases: print and legal requirements, and then not as the only format and through an HTML gateway page that states the page count and file size. Basis: 20 years of observation and 2020 studies for reports on intranets and corporate sites; sample sizes are not given. What transfers: behavior on an intranet is close to that of an employee with a brand book. Uncertainty: a brand book is consulted for specific items and often on a large screen, and designers may work with it differently. (summary) — [Nielsen, Kaley 2020](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/avoid-pdf-for-on-screen-reading/)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] GOV.UK: published 25 June 2025; the update of 24 March 2026 added a link to an accessible version and removed the PDF; the website remained. The former PDF URL returns HTTP 410 Gone (I checked myself). The reason, other than accessibility, is not stated. (summary) — [GOV.UK brand guidelines](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/govuk-brand-guidelines)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] A reverse example of divergence: the Memorial University page was updated on 21 April 2021, while the PDF inside it is dated August 2017. (summary) — [Memorial University Brand Standards](https://www.mun.ca/marcomm/brand/standards/index.php)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] A PDF also lives on as an archival, versioned object: UrbanAIR on Zenodo, v1, 31 October 2025, 5.2 MB, 22 pages. (text) — [UrbanAIR Brand Guidelines](https://zenodo.org/records/17492409)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] There is also a one-page PDF that only points to a website: at Oxford Brookes it is three links (April 2021). (text) — [Oxford Brookes](https://www.brookes.ac.uk/getmedia/6f96563f-d0b8-4b5d-91d9-a61d771f29f7/Visual-Identities-guidelines.pdf)

### Inferences
[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The comparison of media below is built on mechanics and precedents, not on measured use.

| Medium | Strength | Weakness | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| PDF | A single snapshot, print, handoff to a contractor, archive | Poor navigation on screen, copies diverge, accessibility | NN/g, GOV.UK, Memorial |
| Website | Deep links, search, one current version, accessibility | Needs hosting and an owner; invisible to agents when rendered with JS | GOV.UK, Uber |
| Doc or Notion | Cheap start, shared editing | No public examples in my sample; the rest is unchecked | Gap |
| Figma file | Components live next to the designer's work | Closed to non-designers; limits on token import | Kontur, Figma |
| Repository | History, review, diff, CI, machine reading | A barrier for marketing and contractors | GitLab, NHS |
| DAM or portal | Permissions and files in one place | Closed, vendor dependence | Slack, Kontur |
| Binder | Sheet replacement, color samples and originals included | Copies go out of date, distribution is expensive | NASA, NYCTA |

- A defensible recommendation: the human version on an open HTML page with permanent addresses, files in an archive, data in a repository or next to the website, and a PDF only as a dated snapshot. This is an opinion based on precedent, not a result of research.
- The binder's idea outlived the medium: NASA's "register of pages in force" is functionally equal to a changelog and a list of current URLs.

### Gaps
- I found no comparisons of media by how often they are consulted or by errors.
- I could not open a public brand book in Notion or Google Docs: the search ended earlier.
- I did not collect vendor statistics (Marq/Lucidpress, Frontify, zeroheight). It would not have been evidence anyway.
- The British Rail Corporate Identity Manual is not confirmed: the query was not run.

## Length and structure: real ranges, information architecture, one-page brand books

### Takeaway
In a sample of 19 documents the spread runs from one screen to 44 web pages and 174 binder sheets. A skeleton repeats: "foundation, elements, application, resources and contact"; navigation more often follows elements, and channels and tasks are added as a second layer. The sample is biased: public pages are often aimed at partners and press, and internal versions are hidden.

### Inferences

As a replacement for an internal guide it is not confirmed by this sample, because without typography and application it does not cover work on one's own materials.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A practical architecture for the skill author: task cards at the entrance, a tree by elements as the reference, a channel slice only where the values really differ, then an application page, resources, contact.

## Anatomy of a specification page

### Takeaway
A good page sets a measurable value in the unit of its medium, sets clear space relative to a part of the mark, and shows application context and violations with a reason. I found no research on how much specificity is enough; the comparison shows something else: the word "generous" cannot be checked, while "half the height of the icon" can.

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A minimal page template: a number or permanent address, what the element is for, values by channel, an image with markup, context rules, incorrect examples with a reason, where to get the file, who decides exceptions.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A specificity check: can an outsider or a linter say "violated" without a designer? A number in the medium's unit and a relative measure from a part of the mark pass this check, an adjective does not.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Define every variable used in a rule. The ½ S defect on the Shopify page, if the summary is right, shows the cost of an omission.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A value that exists only in a picture is unavailable to copying, to an agent, and to a screen reader. This is a defect of form regardless of design quality.

### Gaps
- I found no experiments on the topic "what precision of a specification reduces errors in applying a brand."
- NASA's physical samples and NYCTA's color sheets cannot be assessed in a digital copy.

## What research on reading and learning says (everything is labeled as transfer)

### Takeaway
None of the studies below examined brand books. Together they support short scannable text, examples, paired comparisons followed by an explicit rule, and separate layers for novice and expert. What was measured was comprehension and learning, not brand compliance at work, so the strength of transfer is moderate.

### Cited Findings
- [TRANSFER] NN/g eye-tracking: on unformatted text the gaze follows an F pattern, the first lines and first words get more attention; formatting softens this; heat maps for 45+ and 47 participants are given. The pattern was described in 2006, updated in 2017. Transfer: the heading and the first word of a rule decide. Uncertainty: the tasks are unrelated to a brand. (summary) — [NN/g 2017](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)
- [TRANSFER] Nielsen 1997: 79% of participants always scanned a new page, 16% read word by word; relative to a promotional control, concise text gave +58% usability, scannable layout +47%, objective language +27%, all together +124%. The article does not state the sample size, the measure is composite, and the year is 1997. Transfer: promotional wording in a brand book hurts finding the rule. (summary) — [Nielsen 1997](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/)
- [EXPERT OPINION] NN/g, flat versus deep hierarchy: other things equal, deep ones are harder, but there is no single right answer. Basis: three hospital sites as an illustration, with no quantitative data; for your own structure it advises card sorting and tree testing. (summary) — [Whitenton 2013](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-vs-deep-hierarchy/)
- [TRANSFER] Minimal manual: 64 first-year students, random assignment, word processing. With the minimal manual they learned faster and better than with the standard one; there is no interaction of manual type and experience. This is a replication of Carroll et al. 1987. (abstract) — [Lazonder, van der Meij 1993, IJMMS 39(5)](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/f811580e-953a-4312-a1ee-9916b3f98e05). Carroll's book "The Nurnberg Funnel" did not open. According to a search summary, one critical source notes that its principles are hard to operationalize; the summary did not say which one. An overview of the theory: [instructionaldesign.org](https://www.instructionaldesign.org/theories/minimalism/). Transfer: start with real tasks, not with brand theory.
- [TRANSFER] Worked examples: Sweller and Cooper 1985 (algebra), the review by Atkinson et al. 2000, the expertise reversal effect by Kalyuga et al. 2003 (an example is redundant and harmful for those who know the material). I did not read the originals, the references are via [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worked-example_effect). According to a search summary: roughly half the time and a fifth of the errors on similar tasks, but the effect does not transfer to changed ones (the likely source is [Matuschak's notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zYHdLJ7TFdpcwGtqDChMNbm), not checked; verify the numbers against the original before using them). Transfer: an application page with a worked example is useful to a novice and a contractor, while a brand designer needs a reference without examples.
- [TRANSFER] Schwartz and Bransford 1998: in three classroom studies students analyzed contrasting cases, then received a lecture or a text; a week later they predicted outcomes more accurately than the control groups. Cases prepare for the explanation and usually do not replace it. (text, abstract and section headings) — [A Time for Telling, Cognition and Instruction 16(4)](https://www.wright.edu/sites/www.wright.edu/files/uploads/2017/Feb/event/Schwartz_Bransford_1998_TimeForTelling.pdf)
- [TRANSFER] Meta-analysis of case comparisons: 57 experiments, 336 tests, d = 0.60 (95% CI 0.47–0.72) in the authors' conference version (text) — [Alfieri, Nokes, Schunn, SREE 2011](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED528947.pdf). In the 2013 paper, per a search snippet from the [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/3708913/Learning_through_Case_Comparisons_A_Meta_Analytic_Review) page, d = 0.50 (0.44–0.56); I did not open the paper itself, and the values diverge. — [Educational Psychologist 48(2), PDF did not open](https://www.lrdc.pitt.edu/Schunn/papers/ContrastingCasesMeta-AlfieriEtAl2013.pdf). According to secondary summaries: a principle after comparison works better, the effect is larger for perceptual material, and searching only for similarities is more effective than searching for similarities and differences. The last point complicates a straightforward reading of do/don't.
- [EXPERT OPINION] Diátaxis: four types of documentation (tutorial, how-to, reference, explanation). The page I read has no empirical evidence: it claims use in hundreds of projects and gives testimonials from Vonage, Gatsby, Cloudflare. The author is not named on the page in the summary (from memory Daniele Procida, not checked). — [diataxis.fr](https://diataxis.fr/)

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A direct projection of Diátaxis onto a brand book: reference (values), how-to (task cards, the file-picking rule), explanation (why it is so), tutorial (a novice's first material). In the brand books read, reference and explanation are the most common; how-to appears at [Supabase](https://supabase.com/brand-assets), [Vercel](https://vercel.com/geist/brands), [Stanford](https://identity.stanford.edu/); [GitHub](https://brand.github.com) singles out Guides and Getting started.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] For do/don't a sensible order is a right and wrong pair, with the explicit rule and reason next to it. This follows from Schwartz and Bransford and from Alfieri's data on the principle after comparison, but there is no experiment on visual identity. The perceptual effect may be stronger for the logo and color than for procedures.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The expertise reversal effect is an argument for layers: a quick reference for the expert, a worked example for the novice.

### Gaps
- I found no studies of reading or application of brand books specifically.
- Larson and Czerwinski 1998 on breadth and depth is not confirmed: the Microsoft page returned 404, and the search ended. Do not use it in the report without checking.
- The direct link to the Alfieri 2013 paper did not open (connection reset and 404), so the moderators come only from secondary summaries.

## Principles from design history and systems thinking (expert positions)

### Takeaway
The historical tradition defends a system of rules that leaves room for variation, not a catalog of prohibitions. These are practitioners' positions, not evidence. I read the texts only in part: Vignelli selectively, Gerstner and Unit Editions only from the publisher's description.

### Cited Findings
- [EXPERT OPINION] Vignelli understands discipline as a set of self-imposed rules and parameters that give the work continuity of intent instead of fragmentation. ## Assets and tools: what to deliver, fonts, color spaces, accessibility

### Takeaway
The practically required minimum: a logo in SVG and PNG by background, a color table with numbers and permitted pairs, a font with a license and a fallback, a contact. WCAG 2.2 sets concrete thresholds; logos are exempt, but the brand's color pairs in text and interface are not.

**Accessibility: the WCAG primary source**
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] WCAG 2.2, version REC-WCAG22-20241212, W3C Recommendation (summary) — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/):
  - SC 1.4.3 (AA): text and images of text at least 4.5:1; large text 3:1. Logo text and brand names are exempt.
  - SC 1.4.6 (AAA): 7:1 and 4.5:1 for large text.
  - SC 1.4.11 (AA): interface elements and meaningful parts of graphics at least 3:1 against adjacent colors.
  - SC 1.4.1 (A): meaning must not be conveyed by color alone.
  - Large text: from 18 pt or from 14 pt bold.
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] W3C clarifications: thresholds are not rounded, 4.499:1 does not pass 4.5:1; 18 pt is 24 CSS px, 14 pt is about 18.5 px; the formula assumes sRGB; the logo exemption does not extend to a logo as an interactive control or to low contrast as a design choice outside brand requirements. Check against the colors set in the code. (summary) — [Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] WCAG 3: a working draft of 10 September 2026, does not replace WCAG 2, the contrast algorithm is not defined, years of work lie ahead. There is no basis yet to build a brand book on APCA as a requirement. (summary, first 100 thousand characters) — [WCAG 3.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag-3.0/)

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A brand book should publish not a palette but a table of permitted pairs with a number and a role: the example above shows that one brand color passes on one background and fails on another. The table should be text, not a picture.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A minimum for a small company (my synthesis, not a standard):
  1. a logo archive with named files: SVG and PNG, color and monochrome, light and dark background, icon;
  2. a color table: role name, HEX and RGB, for print CMYK and Pantone, permitted pairs with contrast;
  3. a font with its license, source, and fallback stated, plus a size scale;
  4. clear space as a relative measure, minimum size in px and mm;
  5. the spelling of the name;
  6. 5–9 incorrect examples with a reason;
  7. 2–3 templates for the company's real tasks;
  8. an owner, a contact, a date, a version;
  9. a token file and a short file for an agent (see the section on the machine-readable layer).
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A font cannot be considered delivered without a license stated: the contractor gets the file but not the right. A fallback font removes part of the problem for partners and agents.

The registry has the highest compliance, 95.08%, with moderate overhead; a pilot with 9 practitioners; six real UIs. The numbers for the other two conditions are not available to me. The small Extended Abstract format. (abstract) — [Cha et al. 2026](https://pure.seoultech.ac.kr/en/publications/design-system-compliant-user-interface-generation-with-llm-agents/), DOI 10.1145/3772363.3798616
- [TRANSFER] Context files in code: AGENTbench 138 tasks from 12 repositories and SWE-bench Lite 300 tasks from 11 repositories, Python, a single run per task. LLM-generated files lowered success by about 0.5% and 2%, human-written ones raised it by about 4%, cost rose by about 20–23% and up to 19%. Agents follow instructions, repository overviews do not help, the files are useful for non-standard practices. The numbers are from v1, the current one is v3 of 29 September 2026, not checked. (summary) — [Gloaguen et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988v1), [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988)
- [TRANSFER] A contradicting result: 10 repositories and 124 PRs, with AGENTS.md the median time is 28.64% lower, output tokens 16.58%, completeness is comparable. A different task and a different measure: efficiency, not benchmark success. (abstract) — [Lulla et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20404)
- [TRANSFER] IFScale: simple keyword-inclusion instructions, up to 500 per prompt, 20 models from seven providers. The best models reach 68% at 500 instructions, with a bias toward earlier ones. Keyword rules are simpler than visual brand rules. (abstract) — [Jaroslawicz et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538)
- [TRANSFER] MOSAIC: up to 20 application constraints, five LLMs. Compliance depends on type, number, and position; there are primacy and recency effects and conflicts between instructions. The numbers are absent from the abstract. — [Purpura et al., EACL 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18554)
- [TRANSFER] GuideBench: 1,272 tasks, 18 LLMs, mostly in Chinese, the best overall accuracy about 87%, in math most are below 60%; rules may contradict the model's common sense. The tool did not find numbers for rule updating. (summary, first 100 thousand characters) — [Diao et al., ACL 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11368v2)
- [EMPIRICAL, adjacent] StyleAdaptedLM: LoRA adapters on brand texts, the authors report growth in style consistency while instruction following is kept. No numbers in the abstract. — [Ramu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18294)
- [EMPIRICAL, weak] Theses: Generator-Validator reduced errors by up to 66% relative to rules in the prompt ([Billinghurst 2024, Malta](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/135370)); a hybrid of regex and LLM is more accurate than a pure LLM ([Jabou 2025, FHNW](https://studierendenprojekte.wirtschaft.fhnw.ch/view/4618)). Search summary only.
- [FACT, practitioner without measurements] Polar Orbit: typed tokens, a closed scale, a ban on bare divs and lint in CI as a contract instead of prose; no results published. — [Polar, 16 June 2026](https://polar.sh/blog/orbit-llm-safe-design-system)
- [EMPIRICAL, adjacent] Analysis of context files: 75.9% contain test procedures, but only 14.8% security and 14.5% performance; the files are hard to read. (abstract) — [Chatlatanagulchai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12884)

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Keep the statuses separate: shipping and working (DTCG 2025.10, Style Dictionary, Figma import, Agent Skills as a format), working beta or preview (Figma MCP in part, Frontify MCP, Storybook MCP, Claude Design), alpha or proposal (DESIGN.md, llms.txt). An effect on brand compliance is not shown for any of them.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The wording "W3C standard" for DTCG is wrong. The correct one: a stable W3C community report.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] For the project's skills the code-task data suggest four rules, with weak transfer: (1) do not drag a story about the company into the agent layer, since overviews did not help; (2) give non-standard rules first, which the model will not guess; (3) put the important things at the start and keep the file short; (4) check a generated file on tasks and do not count the mere fact of its creation as success: in Gloaguen, LLM generation gave a small minus.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] CHI 2026, van den Bosch's survey on templates, and the Polar example converge on one point: it is more reliable to give people and agents a ready component and a check than prose. The grounds are directional, not conclusive: one condition with a number, one survey without a comparison, one practitioner without measurements.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Brand contrast for an agent must be in data. An image grid like GOV.UK's is clear to a person, but a file without pairs, like SKILL.md, leaves the model to choose a pair at its own discretion.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Third parties already publish machine descriptions of other companies' brands from CSS. For a company this is an argument to publish its own canonical file, or agents will take someone else's reconstruction.

## Brand books for people and machines: what to separate into canonical data

### Takeaway
I found no public brand book where a canonical data file demonstrably generates the page for people. There are partial examples: Vercel with a Markdown version, Supabase with tokens and four color spaces, GOV.UK and NHS with functions and variables instead of HEX, GitLab with the source in a repository.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Vercel: the address `/geist/brands.md` returned a version of the page in Markdown, without frontmatter. (summary) — [brands.md](https://vercel.com/geist/brands.md). For Supabase the analogous `.md` returned 404, and its [llms.txt](https://supabase.com/llms.txt) does not mention brand assets. (summary)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Supabase: a page for people with data that is easy for a machine to parse: a token plus values in HEX, RGB, HSL, and OKLCH, files with a purpose. (summary) — [Supabase](https://supabase.com/brand-assets)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] The GOV.UK Design System explicitly asks to reference functions and not to copy HEX, so that a service gets the new palette on update. (summary) — [Design System](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/colour/). NHS says the same about Sass variables. — [NHS](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/design-system/styles/colour)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Atlassian: one source for code, Figma, llms sets, and MCP; but the scope is the design system, and the brand is not mentioned in the file. (summary) — [llms.txt](https://atlassian.design/llms.txt)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] DESIGN.md offers one file for both: YAML for the machine, Markdown for explanation, export to DTCG and Tailwind. (summary) — [Repo](https://github.com/google-labs-code/design.md)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] The Agent Skills format explicitly allows structure: a short instruction, reference files, assets. (page text) — [Spec](https://agentskills.io/specification.md)

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] What to move into the canon and what to leave as prose:

| Canonical data (pages, tokens, the agent file are generated) | Prose and images for people (the agent gets only condensed rules) |
|---|---|
| Colors: role, sRGB HEX, optionally OKLCH and P3 | The meaning and rationale of the brand |
| CMYK and Pantone in an extension: DTCG 2025.10 does not have these spaces | Voice and tone with text examples |
| Permitted pairs with computed contrast and a threshold | Review of successful and unsuccessful work |
| Typography: family, license, fallback, scale, weight | Composition rules settled by judgment |
| Logo: file, variant, background, minimum in px and mm, clear space as a coefficient | Images of incorrect use |
| Name spelling and forbidden forms | History and context of decisions |
| Version, date, owner, contact, license, log | Explanation of changes and migration |

- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A minimal generation scheme: one YAML or `.tokens.json` plus an asset manifest → page, CSS or Sass, Figma tokens, DESIGN.md or SKILL.md. The cost is higher than a Notion page, so a small company can start with a single file, in the spirit of DESIGN.md, but date it and assign an owner. I have not seen a public test of this scheme on a brand book, so this is a hypothesis worth checking on reference tasks.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The deterministic part (contrast, links, permitted values, files) lends itself to linting, as in DESIGN.md. Judgment, such as whether an image is appropriate, does not lend itself to linting and remains with a person.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The machine layer requires static publication. If a page is rendered only on the client, a parallel static file is needed (the Uber and Stitch cases).
