Notes as of 2026-11-06. Goal: decide which brand book sections a small company must have and which can wait.

## How startups and SMEs build a brand in practice, what the founder's role is, and how far the brand carries the founder's values

### Takeaway
The qualitative cases agree: the founder sets the brand's identity, and it is built by trial and error, with minimal planning and small resources. A longitudinal Swedish study shows that at the start the founder's personality and the brand are almost inseparable, and then they diverge as new parties join; this is almost unmeasured quantitatively.

### Cited Findings

**Contradiction.** The New Zealand study shows mature brand management in small firms, whereas the 2005 typology expects most SMEs to sit on the lower rungs ([Wong & Merrilees 2005](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/10610420510601021/full/html), 8 cases, access [ABS]). But the Renton et al. sample is purposive and entrepreneurially active firms were taken, so these are more likely different slices than a refutation.

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] For a small company, the source of the brand already exists when the brand book is created: it is the founder's values and biography. So the agent's first task is not to invent a brand but to draw out explicitly what the founder does implicitly. Indirect support: Fluhrer & Brahm relay Centeno's point that the entrepreneur may not see this link.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The tie between founder and brand is both a risk and an asset. The data on this consist of one longitudinal study and one large correlational study, so I consider a recommendation to separate the founder from the brand at an early stage unfounded.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Almost all the cases are drawn from selected, successful, or visible firms. Failed companies are practically absent from this literature, so a description of practices is not proof that they help.

### Gaps
- There is no quantitative estimate of what share of the brand's identity comes from the founder and what happens to it when staff are hired. Yönel et al. explicitly name the separation of founder and brand as a direction for future research.
- The cases rarely state headcount on the abstract page; checking the full texts of Krake, Centeno, and Spence was not done because of paywalls.

## What small firms do first, what they neglect, which elements matter at which stage, and when formalization is needed

### Takeaway
The best direct work on first steps describes the name, logo, and legal protection as the first visible action, and values, measurement, and brand-aligned hiring as a systematic gap. Stage models exist (at least five), but they are theories built on cases; I found no verified stage prescriptions and no formalization threshold.

### Cited Findings
**What they do first and what they miss: the most concrete study**
- [EMPIRICAL] 15 of 20 invited largest Swiss startups, no more than five years old, 2006 data, 30–150 employees, 5 small and 10 medium-sized, half from ICT, 65% B2B, 10% B2C, about 60% operating internationally. Interviews with 8 CEOs and 7 communications managers plus analysis of logos, websites, and materials. Journal of Product & Brand Management. Access [FT], preprint. Results: — [Bresciani & Eppler 2010](https://sonar.ch/documents/318205/files/bresciani_JPBM_2010.pdf)
  - More than half had a documented view of brand strategy before the company was founded. Most defined a vision, mission, and positioning, but only very few had clear brand values or measurement of results.
  - The first visible step: the name. Half chose suggestive names. Descriptive names created problems: one firm could not protect its full name, another is confused with a different Swiss company. Some already find the name too narrow for the business.
  - Half commissioned a logo from an agency or freelancer. Three firms themselves admit problems with the logo and its protection. Almost all legally protected the name or logo.
  - The website, newsletter, and events dominate. Mass advertising is hardly used, and some firms stopped it because of low returns. Very few have structured hiring aligned with the brand. PR is used little, partly from not knowing its benefit. Partnerships and co-branding exist at only one firm. Six firms have more than one brand.
  - The authors' sequence: brand strategy before founding, then design with a check of the domain and protection, then an activity plan. This is a best-practice recommendation drawn from successful firms without a comparison with unsuccessful ones; the authors themselves name this as a limitation.

**Stage models**
- [EMPIRICAL] Two cases, country not stated on the page (authors and funding Finnish). The corporate brand begins to be built before founding; functions differ by stage; relationship management and use of feedback were added. Journal of Brand Management. Access [ABS]. — [Juntunen, Saraniemi, Halttu & Tähtinen 2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/bm.2010.34)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] The stage names in this work: pre-founding, early growth, effective growth; six functions of the corporate brand: define identity, strategic planning, create identity, consistent communications, engage employees, build image. Access [SECONDARY] through a citation in a review — [Yönel et al. 2026](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/70e2e70f-2212-40ae-b55f-cc1780d4d2ef/content). The stages are also retold in a [CBS note](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/a-blueprint-for-branding-a-business-at-every-stage), not an academic source; it also notes that branding rarely goes in exactly three steps.
- [EMPIRICAL] The brand orientation ladder: minimal, embryonic, integrated; 8 cases, the keyword is Australia. Journal of Product & Brand Management. Access [ABS]. — [Wong & Merrilees 2005](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/10610420510601021/full/html)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Other identity models: no brand, sporadic brand, focused brand (Kusi et al. 2021); hidden, emerging, clarified, possibly adjusted (Törmälä & Gyrd-Jones 2017). Access [SECONDARY] through a review — [Fluhrer & Brahm 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11846-025-00931-9)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Only 2 of 22 studies of identity in SMEs touch on symbolic and visual identity; the link between brand strategy and design is almost unstudied. Access [FT-tool] — [Fluhrer & Brahm 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11846-025-00931-9)

**Funding stage and trademarks**
- [EMPIRICAL, correlation] European startups with venture rounds: the share that filed for a trademark before the round grows from 28% at the seed stage to 53% at the early stage and 72% at the late stage; for patents 10%, 28%, and 44%. A non-peer-reviewed report from agencies with an interest in rights registration. Access [FT], executive summary only. — [EPO/EUIPO 2023](https://link.epo.org/web/publications/studies/en-patents-trade-marks-and-startup-finance-study-executive-summary.pdf)

**The minimum brand concept**
- [EXPERT OPINION] Minimum viable brand: the essay [Yohn 2014 in HBR](https://hbr.org/2014/06/start-ups-need-a-minimum-viable-brand) (a link from the reference list of Yönel et al.; I did not open the page) and a paper involving a venture investor from Project A in [Marketing Review St. Gallen](https://www.econstor.eu/handle/10419/293989) (Walter et al. 2023, access [ABS], design not described on the page, the set of elements is not visible). Yönel et al. call the concept especially useful and at the same time raise the question of how to define and test it, that is, it is untested.

**Contradictions**
- **Do founders neglect the brand at the start?** Yönel et al. relay that many entrepreneurs do not notice branding at first, citing Bresciani & Eppler among others. The primary text of that work shows a mixed picture: a spread of opinions, but also a documented strategy at most firms before founding. The sample is skewed toward the country's largest startups, so both claims may be true for different populations.
- **Define the brand right away or sell first?** Juntunen et al., Bresciani & Eppler, and Yönel et al. advise early definition. One founder (S-13) in Pöhlmann et al. advises sales marketing and data collection first, and a long-term brand strategy only after positive cash flow; this is a single opinion, not a result. For the quantitative data on moderation by age, see the next section; they partly support deferring the investment.
- **PR.** Krake and Petkova et al. consider it suitable for small firms; Swiss startups hardly use it, and the authors call this a sharp contrast with the literature ([Bresciani & Eppler 2010](https://sonar.ch/documents/318205/files/bresciani_JPBM_2010.pdf)).
- **Logos and names.** The review by Yönel et al. recommends descriptive logos (Luffarelli et al. 2019, [SECONDARY]), while Bresciani & Eppler, following Kapferer, warn against descriptive names. These are different objects; the agent must not mechanically carry one over to the other.

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The most expensive mistakes in the cases described involve irreversible decisions: name, protection, domain. Fixing them later costs more than adding a values section. This is the only group of decisions for which the "do it now" argument rests on both the cases and the large (correlational) trademark data.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The values gap deserves attention: firms formulate a vision and positioning but not values, although the literature on the founder's role treats values as the source of the brand. For the agent this is a good starting point.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The figures on shares refer to companies that received venture rounds. They do not show that a typical small company should register a trademark at this stage.

### Gaps
- No comparison was found of what surviving and closed companies do at the start.
- I saw the exact set of stages and functions of Juntunen et al. and the five phases of Centeno et al. only through secondary sources; the full texts are closed.
- There are no data that the order of actions affects the outcome. All the sequences are prescribed by the authors, not tested.

## Do brand orientation and formal branding improve small firms' results: effect sizes, designs, and the strength of causal inference

### Takeaway
Most surveys show a positive, often indirect link, but this is almost always a cross-section with managers' self-assessment, so causation is not established. The effect is probably smaller for young and very small firms, and in one comparison it is zero or negative in an emerging market. I found no meta-analysis of brand orientation in SMEs, and no experiment on branding in small firms either.

**Contradictions and null results**
- Strength of the link: very strong in Wong & Merrilees 2008 and relatively small in Hirvonen et al. 2016. They measure different things: brand performance and business growth.
- Country: Finland positive, Hungary null, emerging markets negative ([Reijonen et al. 2015](https://research.hanken.fi/en/publications/the-impact-of-entrepreneurial-orientation-on-b2b-branding-and-bus/)).
- Consistency: leads to growth through trust in Germany (Eggers et al. 2013); does not directly affect finances in Macedonia (JEEMS 2017).
- Internal branding: the main source of trust in Anees-ur-Rehman et al. 2018 and in Krake's approach; the mediating role is not confirmed in the Fluhrer & Brahm synthesis.
- Overstated wording: Yönel et al. write that corporate branding positively affects the growth of new ventures, citing Batchelor et al. 2025 in the Journal of Brand Strategy; I did not see the design of that work, access [SECONDARY].

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Only the following can be said reliably: firms that consider themselves brand-oriented also consider themselves more successful. The data do not allow claiming that a brand book or brand orientation will raise performance.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Three mechanisms of distortion. Reverse causation: growing firms can afford branding (especially Reijonen 2012). Self-assessment: one manager rates both the brand and the results, and the common-method effect inflates the link. Survival: survivors are surveyed, and the cases pick successful firms (Bresciani & Eppler explicitly, Merrilees by construction).
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Concentration: a significant part of the quantitative data was produced by a small group of Finnish authors on Finnish samples. The independence of these results is lower than the number of papers suggests; transfer to other countries is in question, which the comparison with Hungary confirms.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Moderation by age and size is the most useful signal for the project: the link is stronger in older and larger SMEs, and for young ones it is more useful to watch competitors. This is a moderate argument against investing early in a full brand program, though it does not cancel the definition of the name and identity. The moderation is itself correlational, and older firms may simply accumulate the brand effect for longer.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis of brand orientation in SMEs was found. No experiment, quasi-experiment, or instrumental estimate in which small-firm branding was varied was found; the targeted query for instrumental estimates for trademarks could not be run because of the limit.
- The coefficients of most papers are behind paywalls. This blocks comparison of effect sizes.

### Takeaway

## Legitimacy and reputation of new ventures; symbolic management and storytelling; what trademark studies show

### Takeaway
For new ventures, the brand performs a function that large companies lack: it creates legitimacy with investors and partners. Theory is stronger than data here, but inductive studies and a few quantitative works confirm it. Trademarks are consistently linked to valuations, funding, and growth, but selection is very strong, and a causal effect is not established.

The conceptual work [Fisher, Kotha & Lahiri 2016](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/366324), [SECONDARY], speaks directly of different legitimacy thresholds for different resource providers.
- [EMPIRICAL, correlation] The link of brand performance to growth is stronger in larger and older SMEs than in microenterprises and young ones. An indirect hint that returns accumulate with scale. — [Hirvonen et al. 2016](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/JBIM-11-2014-0217/full/html), as relayed in [Fluhrer & Brahm](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11846-025-00931-9). Brand orientation is linked to firm size: Lin & Siu 2020, [SECONDARY] there too.
- [EMPIRICAL] Agencies: half of the largest Swiss startups commissioned the logo from outside contractors ([Bresciani & Eppler 2010](https://sonar.ch/documents/318205/files/bresciani_JPBM_2010.pdf)). The works found do not study whether bringing in an agency becomes a moment that requires a document.
- [EXPERT OPINION] Participant S-13 advises postponing long-term brand strategies until positive cash flow; S-11 says branding should match the company's phases. Access [FT-tool]. — [Pöhlmann et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-04600-w)
- [EXPERT OPINION] Consultants and investors advise planning and a single message ([Wiesenberg et al. 2020](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/JCOM-10-2019-0129/full/html)). This is the voice of an interested party.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The range of 30–150 employees in [Bresciani & Eppler 2010](https://sonar.ch/documents/318205/files/bresciani_JPBM_2010.pdf) describes the sample, not a threshold. This work cannot be cited as evidence of the headcount at which a brand book is needed.

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] It is better to formulate triggers through events rather than headcount: the moment when the brand starts to be represented by people who are not in daily contact with the founder. Candidates: the first employee who writes on the company's behalf without the founder's review; the first outside contractor; a new market or language; raising investment; a change of owner or the founder's departure. This follows logically from the mechanisms of consistency, but no trigger has been tested.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The agent should not name a threshold headcount. If the user asks "at how many people do we need a brand book", the honest answer is: there are no studies, here are working signs.

### Gaps
- No works were found on the link between the first marketer and brand formalization. From the general organizational literature I recall works on the formal structure of new ventures (Sine, Mitsuhashi & Kirsch 2006) and on scaling (DeSantola & Gulati 2017), but I could not confirm their content in this session; the AOM page is closed and the search limit is exhausted. I build no claims on them.
- No studies were found on what changes when design or content is handed to an agency.

## Which brand book sections a small company must have and which can wait

### Takeaway
No section has causal support, so the split below is an inference and rests on a combination of three criteria: irreversibility of the decision, the presence of descriptive data, and the logic of the mechanism. The mandatory core is short: name and protection, the identity of the founder and company, positioning, a minimal visual kit with written rules, and messages by audience; the rest is added on events.

### Inferences
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] For the agent skill: ask not about headcount but about events: who besides the founder speaks on the company's behalf, whether there are contractors, whether the company is seeking investment, whether a new market is planned.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A small company's brand book is better set up as a short living document with versions, not as a static manual. The analogy from the meta-analysis of planning (combine a plan and learning) and the interviews on agile branding support this but do not prove it.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The skill must not promise growth in performance. It is more accurate to say that the document records irreversible decisions and passes the founder's intent to other people, and also that proof of an effect for small companies is absent.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] For companies without investors and with close customers, the section on story and symbols can be simplified; there the product and relationships matter more (the logic of Petkova et al.).

### Gaps
- The split has not been tested by any work. This is the first testable hypothesis for the project: companies with a minimal core versus companies without a document after 12–24 months.
- The substantive core of a brand book in the sense of Jordá-Albiñana et al. has not been read; it is worth checking against this list.
