## 1. Terminology: how authoritative sources define brand, identity, image, equity, positioning, and which documents they distinguish

### Takeaway

[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] There is no single definition. AMA and Kotler describe a brand as an identifying mark, ISO 10668 as an intangible asset, Keller as knowledge in the consumer's memory. The most dangerous divergence concerns "brand identity": the same term means visual elements, the name and logo, awareness, or the intended content of the brand. I found no normative boundary between "brand book," "guidelines," and "style guide": only the practical literature draws one.

### Cited Findings

**Brand**

- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] On its topic page AMA defines a brand as any distinguishing mark (name, term, design, symbol) that identifies goods or services. The page gives no date for the definition, only © 2026. This is a section page, not an AMA dictionary entry. Access: the whole page. — [AMA, Branding](https://www.ama.org/topics/branding/)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Keller 1993 cites Kotler's 1991 definition: a name, term, sign, symbol, or design, or a combination of them, intended to identify a seller's goods and to differentiate them from competitors'. Keller calls these components "brand identities" and their totality "the brand." Access: full text, the Background section. — Keller, K. L. (1993). Conceptualizing, Measuring, and Managing Customer-Based Brand Equity. Journal of Marketing, 57(1), 1–22. [PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~moorman/Marketing-Strategy-Seminar-2015/Session%203/Keller.pdf)
- [VENDOR] ISO 10668 (2010) defines a brand as a marketing-related intangible asset that may include names, terms, and logos intended to identify goods and to create distinctive images and associations in the minds of stakeholders, which creates economic benefits for the owner. Passed on through an overview by Brand Finance, a company that sells brand valuation. It also notes that the word "brand" is used sometimes as a trademark and sometimes as a package of intellectual property, and that a valuation report must name the specific rights. Access: a vendor overview, not the standard itself. — [Brand Finance, ISO 10668 overview](https://brandfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/1/iso_10668_overview.pdf)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Secondary retellings of ISO 10668 differ in the list of elements: one adds signs, symbols, and design, another limits itself to names, terms, and logos. A university copy of the standard confirms the end of the definition about images, associations, and economic benefits. Access: excerpt. — [copy of ISO 10668, PUCE](https://evaposgrado.puce.edu.ec/maestriasenlinea-2025/pluginfile.php/110106/mod_label/intro/Brand%20valuation%2C%20requirements%20for%20monetary.pdf); [Brand Finance, press release](https://brandfinance.com/press-releases/iso-10668-new-international-standard-on-brand-valuation)
- [EMPIRICAL] de Chernatony and Dall'Olmo Riley reduce definitions of a brand to 12 themes (logo, legal instrument, company, "shorthand," risk reducer, and others) and rely on interviews with experts. **Brand identity: four incompatible meanings**

- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] AMA: visual and symbolic elements that represent the brand (name, logo, color, typography). — [AMA, Branding](https://www.ama.org/topics/branding/)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Keller 1993: "brand identities" as components, that is, the name, logo, and symbol. — [Keller 1993, PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~moorman/Marketing-Strategy-Seminar-2015/Session%203/Keller.pdf)

**Brand image**

- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Keller 1993: the perception of a brand reflected in the associations held in the consumer's memory. Together with awareness, image makes up brand knowledge. — [Keller 1993, PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~moorman/Marketing-Strategy-Seminar-2015/Session%203/Keller.pdf)

**Brand equity**

- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] Keller 1993: the differential effect of brand knowledge on the consumer's response to that brand's marketing, compared with the same marketing-mix element for a fictitious or unnamed product. It arises when the consumer is familiar with the brand and holds "favorable, strong, and unique" associations in memory. — [Keller 1993, PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~moorman/Marketing-Strategy-Seminar-2015/Session%203/Keller.pdf)
- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] AMA: the intangible value of a brand in consumers' minds. — [AMA, Branding](https://www.ama.org/topics/branding/)
- [EXPERT OPINION] Aaker proposes ten attributes of brand strength (differentiation, satisfaction or loyalty, perceived quality, leadership, perceived value, personality, organizational associations, awareness, market share, and market price and distribution coverage) and does not combine them into an overall score. Access: others' description, Wikipedia with a reference to Aaker 1991 and 1996. — [Wikipedia, Brand equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_equity)

**Positioning**

- [PRIMARY-SOURCE FACT] AMA: the definition of an organization's place in the market relative to competitors. — [AMA, Branding](https://www.ama.org/topics/branding/)
- [EXPERT OPINION] Keller, Sternthal, Tybout: positioning requires not only points of difference but also parity with other products, and is built around a frame of reference. ### Inferences

- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] ISO 20671 and ISO 10668 regulate the evaluation and value of a brand, not its creation or documentation. They are useful to the skill as a language for "a brand is an asset," but not as a norm for the structure of a brand book.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Who draws the boundary between documents: judging by what I found, no one does, normatively. A working distinction that can be offered in the report as our convention, not as an industry standard: a style guide describes the rules for applying elements; a design system is the production infrastructure of a digital product and, per NN/g, includes a style guide; guidelines are the rules for brand identifiers; a brand book is guidelines plus a strategic part; a press kit is an external material for the press, not an internal norm.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The skill must set the meaning of "identity" at the start of the work. Otherwise a brief based on Keller 2001 (awareness) and a brief based on Kapferer (content) will produce different documents under one name.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The Russian "брендбук" (brand book) is more often broader than the English "brand guidelines," but this is not a stable distinction, rather a tendency of usage: the same article by Медведицина contains both senses.

## 2. Starting point: what each author thinks comes first and what must exist before the visual and verbal system

### Takeaway

[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The authors name five different beginnings: a system of knowledge in the consumer's memory (Keller), research and audit (Wheeler), competitive alternatives (Dunford), an unchanging ideology (Collins and Porras), and assets as a mechanism of recognition (Romaniuk). The dispute comes down to the axis "outside in" against "inside out." I found no study comparing these sequences with each other, so the order of steps everywhere remains [EXPERT OPINION].

## 3. The minimal strategic core: what repeats across authors and what belongs to one model

### Takeaway

[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Four things repeat across authors: the category or frame of reference, what it is compared with, who it is for, and the reason for choosing it. A fifth, identifiers, is shared by Keller, Wheeler, and Romaniuk. Purpose, personality, archetypes, and the facets of the prism belong to separate frameworks and are not justified as required elements.

### Inferences

- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The minimal input before a brand book for the skill: the category and frame of reference; competitive alternatives; the buyer; the reason for choosing, in which parity is separated from differences; an audit of existing identifiers; a decision on architecture if there is more than one brand. Basis: this is the intersection of the authors, not a tested sufficiency.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Purpose, personality, and archetype should be presented as optional modules, not as required ones. Their evidence base is the weakest, see section 4.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] "Reason for choosing" and "distinctiveness" do not compete for one place. The first answers the question of what to say, the second the question of how to be recognized. The dispute in the section below concerns how important the first is, not whether the second is needed.

### Gaps

- Aaker's Brand Identity model (core and extended identity, four perspectives) was not checked: the query was not run because of the limit. For the report this is a required element, and an additional check is needed.
- A check on whether Kapferer's prism changed in later editions was not run.

## 4. Real disputes and the data on each side

### Takeaway

[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The dispute about differences and distinctiveness is real, but the sides often measure different things, and the main data for "distinctiveness" come from a single institute. The brand personality scale reproduces reliably, but its validity and generalizability are undermined. Purpose branding rests on data selected by outcome; the best work on purpose shows a null main effect and speaks about employees, not buyers.

- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The sides of the differentiation dispute measure different things. ## 6. Brand architecture: Olins, the Aaker and Joachimsthaler spectrum, the moment it is needed

### Takeaway

[RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Olins (monolithic, endorsed, branded structure) and Aaker and Joachimsthaler (a spectrum from branded house to house of brands) describe one continuum at different levels of detail. There is correlational support for a corporate brand in large American companies, but there is no published threshold at which a company needs an architecture. For a single brand it is a default decision, not a separate piece of work.

### Cited Findings

- [EXPERT OPINION] Olins (1989, Corporate Identity: Making Business Strategy Visible through Design): three structures, monolithic, endorsed (example General Motors), and branded (example Unilever). Access: others' description, an excerpt from WARC. — [WARC, The Story of Shell](https://www.warc.com/content/article/mkt/the-story-of-shell/en-gb/79636)
- [EMPIRICAL] Melewar, Saunders, and Balmer tested Olins's model on British companies in Malaysia: with the exception of the slogan, the degree of standardization of visual identity matches his structure; companies with a well-known name more often use the monolithic structure. The abstract does not name the sample size. The article describes only the visual system, not portfolio strategy. Access: abstract. — Melewar, T. C., Saunders, J. & Balmer, J. M. T. (2000). The saliency of Olins visual identity structure in relation to UK companies operating in Malaysia. Corporate Reputation Review, 3(3), 194–200. [Brunel](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/1711)
- [EXPERT OPINION] Aaker and Joachimsthaler: architecture as a way of organizing the portfolio and the links between brands; a spectrum of four basic strategies and nine substrategies, with the position on it depending on how much the brand drives the purchase. Examples: P&G (house of brands), Virgin (branded house), endorsed brands with a small endorser role. By the excerpt the evidence looks like cases, but the text was not read: the copy on Novasbe returned 404. Access: abstract through an excerpt. — Aaker, D. A. & Joachimsthaler, E. (2000). The Brand Relationship Spectrum: The Key to the Brand Architecture Challenge. California Management Review, 42(4), 8–23. [CMR](https://cmr-mig.berkeley.edu/2000/08/42-4-the-brand-relationship-spectrum-the-key-to-the-brand-architecture-challenge); [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/000812560004200401)
- [EMPIRICAL] Rao, Agarwal, and Dahlhoff: 113 American firms (23% of the S&P 500, about 38% of its value), 1996–2000, Tobin's q. Corporate branding is linked to a higher q, and house of brands and a mixed strategy to a lower one; advertising raises q for corporate brands and lowers it for houses of brands; most firms could improve q with a different strategy. The authors themselves note that the sample is not representative of the whole economy, that the analysis is at the firm level, and that the coding of strategies is crude. The firms are noticeably larger than the rest, and the data cover a period of high market valuations. Access: full text of the working version (2003); the journal version is listed as Journal of Marketing, 68(4), 126–141, 2004, and I did not read it. — [MSI 03-126](https://thearf-org-unified-admin.s3.amazonaws.com/MSI/2020/06/MSI_Report_03-126.pdf)
- [EMPIRICAL] A Brazilian replication gave coefficients noticeably different from the originals, that is, the result is not universal. Access: excerpt, I did not read the details. — [PUC-Rio](https://www.maxwell.vrac.puc-rio.br/10150/10150_6.PDF)
- [EMPIRICAL] Hsu, Fournier, and Srinivasan reproduce and extend Rao and coauthors, adding sub-branding and endorsed branding and refining the mixed architecture. I do not know the results. Access: excerpt. — Hsu, L., Fournier, S. & Srinivasan, S. (2016). Brand architecture strategy and firm value. Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science, 44(2), 261–280. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11747-014-0422-5)
- [EXPERT OPINION] In Romaniuk's book a separate chapter is devoted to the hierarchy of corporate and parent brands and sub-brands, that is, the architecture question also arises at the level of assets. I did not see the chapter's content. — [Schweitzer, table of contents](https://www.schweitzer-online.de/en/buch/Romaniuk/Building-Distinctive-Brand-Assets/9780190311506/A43363445/)

### Inferences

- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] Olins describes the visible structure, while Aaker and Joachimsthaler address the question of the brand's role in the purchase. So Olins's typology suits the brand book section on the visual system, while the spectrum is better suited to portfolio strategy. Melewar and coauthors tested precisely the visual part.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] A practical threshold that can be offered as a heuristic but not as an established fact: architecture becomes a separate piece of work when a second product appears with a different audience or reputational risk, an acquisition, a sub-brand, a partner or endorsed brand, or an entry into a market under a different name. Until then a single line "one brand for everything" and a rule for when to revisit it are enough.
- [RESEARCHER INFERENCE] The conclusions of Rao and coauthors cannot be transferred to startups or small business: the sample consists of very large companies, the link is correlational, and the Brazilian replication weakens it.

### Gaps

- The Brand Handbook (Olins, 2008) was not checked. It may contain a later wording of the typology.
- I found no published threshold of size or number of products at which an architecture is needed. The search was incomplete, so this is not proof of absence.
- No comparisons of architectures for small and B2B companies were found.
