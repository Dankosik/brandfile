# Anatomy of a good brand book: what is proven and what is not

Updated: November 6, 2026.
Status: a synthesis of two-pass desk research carried out by AI agents, for a team that builds skills for AI agents. The section table and all recommendations remain our inference; they have no causal test.

## Conclusion

**What a small company must have.** We consider a short core of seven parts mandatory. A name, checked for domain and protection. One page of meaning: the category, who you are compared with, who it is for, the reason to choose it, what the company is not, how to describe it in one sentence and in one paragraph, and in B2B also who will work with the client. A few founder decisions that are not up for debate, instead of a manifesto. A minimal system: logo files, clear space (the free space around the mark) and minimum size, a color table with allowed pairs, a typeface with its license and a fallback, wrong examples with the reason. A short voice block: who speaks, «вы» or «ты» (formal or informal "you") and letter case by channel, three principles of the form "X, not Y", bans for bad situations, a few before/after pairs. Two or three working templates for real tasks. An owner, a contact, a date, and a version.

**What can wait.** Everything else is switched on by an event, not by headcount. A full voice page is needed when another person or a model starts writing on the company's behalf without the founder's review. A full visual system is needed with the first contractor, brand architecture with a second product that has a different audience or risk, co-branding with the first partnership, localization with a new market or language, motion, sound, and environment when video, sound, or physical locations appear. Archetypes, the Kapferer prism, personality scales, and long manifestos can be skipped: the structure of some of these frameworks has been tested, but no one has shown a benefit from working with them. The table with the basis for each row is in section 8.

**What a brand book should look like.** An open HTML page or a small site with permanent addresses; a PDF only as a dated snapshot. At the entrance, the reader's tasks; under them, a reference by element; a cut by channel only where the values differ. Every rule is checkable: a number in the medium's unit or a relative measure instead of an adjective. Values are written as text, not as an image. Colors are given as a table of allowed pairs with contrast, wrong examples are explained with a reason, and what is fixed is separated from what can change. Next to it lie the files, the templates, and one data file that can be read without a browser. Judging by the samples, the lower bound of size is a few thousand words, not 80 pages.

**What this rests on.** This is our inference, not a research result. The queries we ran found not a single peer-reviewed study of written brand documents in small firms, no comparison of media, no usage logs, and no test of a headcount threshold. For large organizations there is one direct dataset: a survey of 686 employees of 20 Dutch organizations (2000–2001), self-reported, with perceived consistency as the outcome and no causal conclusion. The table rests on three supports: how irreversible a decision is, the mechanism for passing knowledge to other people, and how often a practice repeats in real documents.

**The other answers in brief.** A good brand book passes one test: an unfamiliar contractor can use it to make new material that is recognized as yours. Brand work starts before the brand book, and the schools disagree on where exactly; the corporate school, instead of a starting point, looks for gaps between vision, culture, and image; the order of steps has not been tested anywhere. The claim "a brand book gets forgotten because it is a boring PDF" has no evidence. The best available data, all correlational, point to leaders' example, up-to-date rules, and knowledge of the strategy, while clarity and working samples are supported only by adjacent fields. Voice cannot be reduced to adjectives: the confirmed effects are narrow and conditional. Informality hurts an unfamiliar brand and when stakes are high. On service failure there is no real dispute; the studies measure different things: caution is sensible toward the affected customer, and observers of a public complaint do not punish friendly humor. Figures like "+33% revenue from consistency" must not be repeated (section 5).

Every substantive claim is labeled. In tables the label sits in a separate column or in the table caption.

| Label | What it means | How to treat it |

| `[study]` | An empirical result; the design and sample are given next to it | A correlation is called a correlation. We claim causation only with randomization |
| `[transfer]` | A study from an adjacent field, not about brand books | Next to it we say what transfers and what remains in question |
| `[expert]` | An expert's opinion, including famous unverified frameworks; press and Wikipedia retellings also go here | Fame does not replace verification; a retelling is weaker than the primary source |
| `[vendor]` | A claim by a party that sells a related product or service, including agencies | The seller's interest is visible in advance |

The second word in a label marks the access level. `text`: the researcher read the extracted text from a browser or PDF themselves, not necessarily all of it. `abstract`: only the abstract or the record card. `description`: a third-party retelling, a search snippet, or a table of contents. `digest`: the page came back from a tool that returns a retelling by a helper model, so numbers and wording must be checked before quoting. `archive`: the text was read only as an Internet Archive copy; the snapshot date means "no later than," and we did not see the live state of the page. The `[document]` label with no qualifier means digest. "Not found" everywhere means "not found in the queries we ran" (section 10).

The material was collected by AI agents in two passes. Below, "researcher" means the agent that ran one of the seven tracks of the first pass or one of the four of the second, and "notes" means these agents' working files in the folders `docs/research/notes/brand-book-anatomy/` and `docs/research/notes/brand-book-anatomy-second-pass/`. No human rechecked the sources; what was checked separately is stated in section 10.

## 1. The word "brand book" guarantees nothing: Figma uses it for a file only about the logo

`[inference]` The researchers found no normative boundary between a brand book, guidelines, and a style guide: only practitioner literature draws it. ISO 10668 and ISO 20671 deal with brand valuation and evaluation, not with creating or documenting a brand, and we could not read the standards themselves (`[vendor; description]` [Brand Finance overview](https://brandfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/1/iso_10668_overview.pdf), [BSI](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/brand-evaluation-principles-and-fundamentals)).

The basic terms are defined in different ways. `[document]` AMA describes a brand as a distinctive feature that identifies goods, and identity as its visual and symbolic elements ([AMA](https://www.ama.org/topics/branding/)). `[expert; text]` Keller, following Kotler, calls a brand a combination of name, sign, and design, and defines brand image as perceptions reflected in associations in the consumer's memory. Brand equity, for him, means the difference in response to the marketing of this brand compared with the same action for an unnamed product ([Keller 1993](https://people.duke.edu/~moorman/Marketing-Strategy-Seminar-2015/Session%203/Keller.pdf)).

The most dangerous word is "identity." For AMA it is the logo and color; in Keller 1993 it is the name and symbol; in [Keller 2001](https://thearf-org-unified-admin.s3.amazonaws.com/MSI/2020/06/MSI_Report_01-107.pdf) the first step, "establish identity," is equated with awareness; for Kapferer, according to secondary descriptions, it is the brand's content on the sender's side (`[expert; description]` [Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/kapferer-brand-identity-prism/)); for Aaker it is an intended set of associations with a core and an extension (`[expert; digest]` [Prophet 2011](https://prophet.com/2011/03/5-aakers-top-ten-brand-precepts/)). `[inference]` Briefs with different meanings produce different documents under the same name, so the meaning has to be set at the start of the work.

In Russian the word is broader, but loose. `[document]` Medveditsina, in a single paragraph, calls a brand book a set of visual standards and right away includes values and mission in it ([CyberLeninka](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/formirovanie-otnosheniy-brenda-s-potrebitelem)). `[vendor]` Practical materials give the ladder «логобук, гайдлайн, брендбук» (logobook, guideline, brand book) and admit that in the professional community «брендбук» has become a synonym for «гайдлайн» ([Sostav](https://www.sostav.ru/blogs/275048/47066), [Netology](https://netology.ru/blog/10-2024-what-is-guideline)).

Below is our working convention, not an industry standard `[inference]`: guidelines describe the rules for brand identifiers, a brand book adds a strategic part to them, a style guide sets the rules for applying elements, visual or verbal, and a design system serves as the production infrastructure of a digital product. The examples in the table are `[document]`; the assignment to a genre is ours.

| Genre | What question it answers | Who the reader is | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand book | Who we are, for whom, and how to create new things | Own team and contractors writing on the company's behalf | [PostHog](https://posthog.com/handbook/brand/foundations), [GitLab](https://design.gitlab.com/brand-introduction) |
| Visual guideline | What the brand looks like and how to create new materials, without meaning | Material author, contractor | [Zapier](https://brand.zapier.com/principles), [IBM Design Language](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/) |
| Logo page | Which file to take and what not to do with the mark | Outside author, partner, lawyer | [Linear](https://linear.app/brand), [Supabase](https://supabase.com/brand), [Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/brand) |
| Press kit | What to write about the company and which materials to take | Journalist | [Stripe](https://stripe.com/newsroom/brand-assets), [Buffer](https://buffer.com/press), [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/brand) |
| Partner rules | How we, as a third party, avoid infringing rights | Partner, developer, licensee | [Zapier](https://partnerportal.zapier.com/sys/document/open/7Ot00000000003a00hE), [Apple](https://www.apple.com/legal/intellectual-property/guidelinesfor3rdparties.html), [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/company/legal/tmcusageguidelines/) |
| Writing guide | How we write | Text author | [Monzo](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice/), [Mailchimp](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/), [T—Zh](https://docs.google.com/document/d/14XdGIjVJLM_FsjHzyh5ca8PkffngykzXd2bLPHzA2ME/pub) |
| Design system | Which components and tokens to use in the product | Product designer and developer | [Kontur](https://guides.kontur.ru/), [Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations), [USWDS](https://designsystem.digital.gov/about/whats-new/) |

We count as a brand book a document with four features `[inference]`: it has meaning (at least two sections out of mission, positioning, audience, personality); it has a system (the logo and two more elements); it has rules for creating new things, not only bans; its addressee is internal. The title does not show this. `[document; text]` The Figma Brand Book consists of 11 slides and 686 words, only about the marks ([PDF](https://static.figma.com/uploads/6645755656177435f83e10b48d9947645b55a033)). `[document]` The «Брендбук» ("Brand book") on the Litres page is a set of archives and PDFs ([Litres](https://www.litres.ru/cms/11127/)); the PDF itself was not read.

The boundaries are blurred. `[document]` Dodo publishes a mission, an audience matrix, and a manifesto, moves the detailed rules to another site, and calls the page a book for one evening ([Dodo](https://brandbook.dodopizza.info/)); the rules site itself, Shift, turned out to be the team's working base: logo, tone, editorial policy, and about 30 topical sections (`[document; archive]` [2025 snapshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20251209162934/https://shift.dodobrands.io/en/)). Monzo and T—Zh do without logo and color, but give a text author more than many "brand books" do. `[inference]` So the quick test is this: can an unfamiliar contractor use the document to make a new layout and a new text that are recognized as yours? A logo page passes only the "do not infringe" test; a press kit passes neither.

## 2. Work starts before the brand book, and the order of steps has not been tested anywhere

Authors name different starting points. `[inference]` The dispute is usually described with the axis "outside-in" versus "inside-out"; `[expert; abstract]` that is how [Urde, Baumgarth, Merrilees 2013](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbrese:v:66:y:2013:i:1:p:13-20) describe it, before moving away from the opposition toward a hybrid. The corporate school gives a third answer: look not for a starting point but for gaps between versions of the company. `[expert; digest]` In [Hatch, Schultz 2003](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/03090560310477654/full/html) these are gaps between vision, culture, and image; `[expert; description]` in [Balmer, Greyser 2002](https://cmr.berkeley.edu/2002/05/44-3-managing-the-multiple-identities-of-the-corporation) five identities, in [Urde 2013](https://econbiz.de/Record/the-corporate-brand-identity-matrix-urde-mats/10010234941) a matrix of views from the inside, from the outside, and through the core. The support here is case studies of large organizations; we have not seen a test showing that closing the gaps improves results. `[inference]` For a small company this gives a simple step: compare what the founder says, what the team does, and what customers hear.

**No comparison of these sequences with one another was found, so the order of steps remains opinion everywhere** `[inference]`. The direct consequence of the Wheeler and Meyerson book: the brand book stands at the end of the process. This is the book's logic, not a proven dependency of the result. The second pass ran no separate query on such comparisons. The books remain unread, and Kapferer, Sharp, Olins, and Holt were not checked in their own texts, so their positions are not described here, except for the marked secondary retellings.

**The recurring core.** `[inference]` A summary of Keller, Dunford, Wheeler, and Romaniuk gives four shared things: the category or frame of reference; who you are compared with; who it is for; the reason to choose. Within the reason to choose, Keller and coauthors separate parity, that is, the minimum required for the category, from differences. The fifth, identifiers, is shared by Keller, Wheeler, and Romaniuk. This is an overlap of authors, not tested sufficiency. Purpose, personality, archetypes, and the facets of the prism belong to separate models. In B2B, two studies raise the weight of the organization and its people: buyers of one technology in [Kuhn et al. 2008](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/13522750810845540/full/html) and a scale built on 421 IT services executives in [Coleman et al. 2011](https://research.birmingham.ac.uk/en/publications/b2b-service-brand-identity-scale-development-and-validation/), both `[study; description]`. `[inference]` If the buyer chooses an organization, the section "who we are and who will work with you" stops being optional.

**What small firms do.** `[study; text]` The best direct study covers the 15 largest Swiss startups, 2006, with interviews and analysis of materials. More than half had a documented view of brand strategy even before founding, but very few had clear values and measurement. The first visible step was the name, and descriptive names created problems with protection and confusion ([Bresciani, Eppler 2010](https://sonar.ch/documents/318205/files/bresciani_JPBM_2010.pdf)). There is no comparison with unsuccessful firms, and the sample is skewed toward the most visible companies. `[study; abstract]` Based on interviews with 35 owners from 30 firms, small firms build a brand by trial and error with minimal planning ([Centeno et al. 2013](https://pureportal.strath.ac.uk/en/publications/the-five-phases-of-sme-brand-building/)). `[study; digest]` A longitudinal study of 10 Swedish firms shows that at the start the brand and the founder's personality are almost inseparable, and then they diverge as customers, partners, and new owners get involved ([Astner, Gaddefors 2025](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/QMR-06-2023-0085/full/html)). `[inference]` For a small company the source of the brand usually already exists, so the first task is to draw out what is implicit, not to invent something new.

### Three real disputes

**Differentiation or distinctiveness.** `[study; description]` The Ehrenberg-Bass school shows a low level of perceived differentiation among competing brands ([retelling in a Bournemouth manuscript](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/38862/1/Revised%20Manuscript%20-%20Clean%20Copy.pdf), [Sharp on BrandZ data](https://byronsharp.com/blog/files/millwardbrown_confirmation.html)); the journal texts were not read. The original article of this line, Differentiation or Salience, was published in 1997 by Scriven, Barnard, and Ehrenberg ([WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/article/jar/differentiation-or-salience/en-gb/9076), `digest`); a reference that turns up to Ehrenberg, Barnard, Sharp 2000 was not confirmed. `[study; abstract]` The Y&R panel argues against them: changes in differentiation point to future accounting results ([Mizik, Jacobson 2008](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/financial-value-impact-perceptual-brand-attributes)). Answers about associations are, moreover, systematically linked to past use of the brand across 45 datasets ([Romaniuk et al. 2012](https://eprints.kingston.ac.uk/23149/); `[study; description]`), which weakens the survey measures of both sides.

No direct test of assets versus differentiation turned up in the queries we ran, but independent studies have tested differentiation against outcomes; we have not seen the coefficients of any of them. `[study; digest]` [Datta et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1509/jm.15.0340): 290 brands, 25 packaged-goods categories, ten years of scanner data, and the BAV survey. Relevance, esteem, and knowledge are positively linked to sales-based equity, differentiation weakly negatively; according to the [AMA](https://www.ama.org/2017/12/20/are-you-managing-brand-equity-incorrectly) retelling, which contradicts itself in places, differentiation matters in concentrated and experience categories, and differentiated brands gain more from advertising and less from discounts. `[study; description]` [Stahl et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1509/jm.10.0522): the US car market, ten years of customer data and BAV. Brand equity is linked to higher profitability but lower acquisition and retention; which BAV pillar each of these effects belongs to is not confirmed. `[inference]` These are correlations on one instrument, without B2B. A working hypothesis, not a fact: differentiation works for price and margin more than for volume. For a brand book the dispute is milder than it looks: identifiers need managing in both cases, and the weight of the reason to choose depends on the category and the goal.

**Brand personality.** `[study; text]` [Aaker 1997](https://gwern.net/doc/economics/1997-aaker.pdf) derived five factors from 631 questionnaires and 37 brands, and noted the limits for other cultures herself. `[study; abstract]` [Austin et al. 2003](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/a-re-examination-of-the-generalizability-of-the-aaker-brand-perso/) advise great caution in applying the scale to an individual brand. [Avis et al. 2014](https://www.otago.ac.nz/marketing/profiles/the-brand-personality-of-rocks), using the same logic, obtained a distinct "personality" for photographs of rocks; this is a deliberately provocative argument about method, not proof that the concept is useless. `[study; text]` The meta-analysis [Wang, Heinberg, Eisend 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.22216) (95 articles, 1441 effects, antecedents only) finds significant links with all factors except consumer self-confidence. The strongest link is with the respondent's own personality: r = 0.462, interval 0.297–0.628; in the 2013 meta-analysis it was 0.100, and we do not explain this gap. Judging by a text search, the critique by Avis and coauthors is not discussed there. `[study; digest]` Across 76 samples, sincerity and competence give the largest effects on attitude and purchase intention; the page gives no values ([Eisend, Stokburger-Sauer 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11002-013-9232-7)). `[inference]` The links are measured, but these are self-report correlations, and the dispute over what exactly they measure is not settled. Personality works as a metaphor for voice and as a design decision; there is little ground for using it to measure an individual brand.

**Purpose.** `[study; text]` The best independent study covers 456,666 responses from employees of 429 US public companies: the purpose measure itself is not linked to financial results; only the combination of high purpose and clarity from leadership is; the authors say directly that causation is not established ([Gartenberg et al. 2019](https://ecgi.global/sites/default/files/Corporate%20Purpose%20and%20Financial%20Performance-%20Paper.pdf)). `[vendor]` Claims of manyfold growth come from brand owners and ranking authors ([Unilever in Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/unilever-praises-purpose-investor-criticism/)), and `[expert]` Sharp takes the Stengel data apart as selection on the outcome ([analysis](https://marketingscience.info/the-flawed-stengel-study-of-business-growth)). `[inference]` Gartenberg measures employees' perception of the meaning of their work and does not transfer to purpose marketing. No peer-reviewed test of "start with why" turned up in the two queries we ran.

**What has been tested by measurement.** `[study; abstract]` Characters and logos have greater potential for unique ownership than color: 1281 elements, 13 categories, 19 countries ([Ward et al. 2020](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/jobman/v27y2020i4d10.1057_s41262-020-00187-6.html)). `[study; description]` Marketers judge their own assets poorly ([Marketing Week on Ehrenberg-Bass work](https://www.marketingweek.com/marketers-judgement-strength-brand-assets-rarely-accurate/)). These studies measure the link between an asset and the brand, not sales. `[study; description]` The well-known 996 IPA cases in Binet and Field (self-selected award entries; only a retelling was read) concern advertising budgets, not brand content ([summary](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/les-binet-and-peter-field-the-long-and-the-short-of-it)).

**What about the frameworks.** `[inference]` No direct comparisons of companies that do and do not work with these frameworks turned up, but the structure of some frameworks has been tested. The structure of the prism is confirmed in two narrow studies: [Viot 2011](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/10610421111134941/full/html) (`digest`) links it to the acceptance of extensions, and [Barros et al. 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/ijilea/v20y2016i2p214-231.html) (`description`, 235 students) write that there is no reliable identity scale yet. An adapted CBBE pyramid was tested with structural modeling in particular contexts ([Yu et al. 2008](https://journal.hep.com.cn/fbr/EN/PDF/10.1007/s11782-008-0031-6), `description`), but the hierarchy as a mandatory order, as far as we can see, was not. For the Brand Report Card, no test turned up in the one query we ran; archetypes, Wheeler's five phases, and the Dunford model were not tested. The advice to skip these frameworks now rests on a different ground: no one has shown that working with them benefits a small company.

Brand architecture in this literature is correlational and built on very large companies (`[study; text]` 113 American firms, [Rao et al.](https://thearf-org-unified-admin.s3.amazonaws.com/MSI/2020/06/MSI_Report_03-126.pdf)); no published threshold at which it becomes necessary was found.

## 3. Anatomy: public documents most often protect the mark, while meaning is rare or behind a login

Below are the data of two independent audits from the first pass, which cannot be merged into one percentage; the second-pass documents are described separately under the table. The first: 24 sets of documents from startups, small companies, and scaleups, 11 of them from small and young ones, on a "yes, partly, no" scale, without counting the cells where a separate document was not read. The second: 19 sources from large organizations in three genres (brand standards 7, external rules 8, design systems and style 4), and its soft count includes even a navigation item whose contents no one read. Each was tagged by a single AI agent, mostly from digests, and neither is random. In the first audit, voice documents were added on purpose, so their share there is inflated. That is why the table shows orders of magnitude `[inference]`.

**How often a section appears in a public document shows what an outside reader needs, not what matters to the brand.** A journalist and a partner need files and terms of use, so these are at the top. Companies usually do not publish their internal strategy. `[document]` Red Hat keeps its brand position behind a login and its Corporate Style Guide behind a VPN ([Platform](https://www.redhat.com/en/about/brand/standards/platform), [Co-branding](https://www.redhat.com/en/about/brand/standards/co-branding/red-hat-led)). `[document; text]` Kontur shows this most directly: the brand platform, customer segments, and voice are visible in the menu, and inside there is a request to sign in ([brand book](https://in.kontur.ru/brandbook), [platform](https://in.kontur.ru/brandbook/platform)). `[study; text]` Of the 100 Spanish companies in the 2018 Merco ranking, 21 had a brand center, and 18 of those closed it to outsiders ([Fernández Rincón, Hellín Ortuño 2019](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/7240338.pdf)). Counterexamples: Dodo, PostHog, Mozilla, and Selectel (`archive`) publish their meaning. `[inference]` A section template cannot be derived from these frequencies.

The first pass explained the portals' empty responses by client-side rendering, but the causes differ: Netflix and the Mailchimp portal are behind a login, while IBM Design Language and Mozilla are open and simply could not be read without a browser (section 10).

**The distribution by genre matters more than the count of sections.** In the first audit there are only two real brand books (PostHog and GitLab) and one hybrid (Dodo), four writing guides, one design system, 15 logo pages and press kits, and one partner document. Among small and young companies, logo pages and press kits make up 10 documents out of 11 `[inference]`. So the phrase "look at Google's brand book" usually means "look at the rules for partners," and only the brand standards group, not all 19 sources, can be compared with startups.

**The second pass added brand books, but not to the small subgroup.** It read 13 documents that the first pass had not opened: IBM, Mozilla, NHS, Mastercard, Netflix, Uber 2020, Starbucks, Slack, Zapier, Notion, Selectel, Shift, Kontur. They were chosen as unread and suspected to be rich, one agent tagged them, and five are known from the archive, so they do not add up with the 24 and the 19. By the four features of section 1, `[document; text]` [Mozilla](https://brand.mozilla.com/document/240#/-/who-we-are) and `[document; archive]` [Selectel](https://web.archive.org/web/20240329120242/https://brand.selectel.ru/) pass (no voice, templates closed, read partially); Kontur passes with the caveat that its meaning is behind a login. All three are outside the small subgroup, so the estimate of 10 out of 11 does not change; in the large group there are probably more real brand books than the first pass showed.

Among the eight new large documents, all have a logo, 5 have voice (three only in the archive), 5 have mission or values (two mentions and a menu label), 6 have motion, 3 each have sound and environment, 3 have audience, 2 have personality, 1 has positioning. `[inference]` The earlier thesis softens only for rich brand standards portals: voice and values appear there more often, while positioning and audience are rare everywhere.

**What is inside the full samples.** `[document]` PostHog: taste, personality with a "yes/no" table, audience, product description, reasons to choose, "what we are not," then logo, color, typeface, illustrations, animation, photos, screenshots, charts ([foundations](https://posthog.com/handbook/brand/foundations), [visual identity](https://posthog.com/handbook/brand/visual-identity)). They give the author a criterion, while the files only remove friction. `[document; text]` Mozilla opens with the brand promise, positioning, mission, and voice with two modes of writing ([Voice & Tone](https://brand.mozilla.com/document/240#/-/voice-tone)).

**The most direct academic trace is unavailable.** `[study; abstract]` An analysis of 341 identity manuals divides content into a core and a periphery and shows differences by culture, product type, and scale; the authors call the conclusions preliminary because of possible type I errors, and the composition of the core is behind a paywall ([Jordá-Albiñana et al. 2009](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/02651330910950411/full/html)). The second pass found no open copy, so our table still cannot be checked against it. `[study; text]` The only numbers on composition that were read: among 114 people involved, from 20 Dutch organizations (2000–2001), the rules covered colors for 95%, the logo for 86%, typefaces for 81%, grids for 65%, while language and tone were part of the framework for 24% ([van den Bosch 2005](https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/files/6087523/thesis_A.vandenBosch.pdf), pp. 53–59). These are opinions, not an analysis of documents.

**What was in historical standards.** Only the NASA manual was read from the document itself; the others are known from secondary sources. `[document; text]` The [NASA scan](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/nasa_graphics_manual_nhb_1430-2_jan_1976.pdf) has 60 pages and nine parts: the logo with nine wrong applications, reproduction material, stationery, forms, publications, signage, vehicles, awards, appendices. There is no strategy, except for the administrator's letter. `[expert; archive]` The list of British Rail sheets on a collector's site shows four volumes from 1965–1970, dated sheets marked Obsolete, and sheet lists from 1969 and 1976 ([2023 snapshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20231209155303/https://www.doublearrow.co.uk/manual.htm)); the scans were not opened. `[inference]` These manuals answer the question "how to produce it." Over half a century, voice, motion, sound, and accessibility were added, while strategy was absent from the public part then and is rare now.

## 5. A boring PDF does not explain why brand books are forgotten: for large organizations there is one self-reported dataset, for small firms no data were found

**What exists directly.** We found no portal logs, no comparison of formats, no audit of released materials, and no studies of agencies as brand book users. Direct data exist only for large organizations, and that is one Dutch project: the dissertation [van den Bosch 2005](https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/files/6087523/thesis_A.vandenBosch.pdf) and the articles [2004](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/managing-corporate-visual-identity-use-and-effects-of-organizatio/), [2006](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/03090560610670034/full/html), [2006](https://doi.org/10.1177/0021943605285476), four publications about one survey. `[study; text]` 686 questionnaires, 20 organizations with 400 or more employees, from November 2000 to May 2001; respondents were selected by contact persons, response rate 48%. The outcome is consistency as the employees themselves see it, not an audit of materials. The strongest links are with leaders' example (β = .22) and up-to-date rules (.18), then a visual identity manager (.16) and preferred suppliers (.14); access to the rules and the topic in onboarding of newcomers are weak (.08 each); templates, detailed rules, a help desk, and consultations did not enter the model. Adjusted R² = .26; the number of measures applied, if added, becomes the strongest predictor (β = .37; example .14, up-to-dateness .10). "No" and "don't know" are merged, so a predictor means that the employee knows about the measure; between 15 and 36% did not know about each measure. The scale of tools and support correlates with consistency at r = .60, socialization at .58, knowledge of strategy at .55; R² = .50 in the structural model is probably inflated: two paths were added by modification indices, and the scales overlap.

`[inference]` These are correlations from a single self-report; causation is not shown. Leaders' example comes first, but it cannot be called decisive: up-to-date rules are right next to it, although in manufacturing, where there are only four organizations, their β = .00. The document was compared only through employees' answers: up-to-dateness is linked, accessibility weakly, detail not at all. The quality of the document was not independently assessed or varied, so the question of whether a good brand book is needed remains unanswered. Van den Bosch herself considers the rules "necessary, but not sufficient" (2005, p. 140). This does not transfer directly to small companies: 400 or more employees, more than half of the organizations nonprofit, the data 25 years old, the main medium paper.

`[study; text]` The same project has focus groups in three organizations and audits in three more: the rules went out of date, diverged, and did not reach people, and templates were not installed. The same complaints were heard in the most consistent organization and in the least consistent one; what differed was planned implementation, a coordinator, and a budget. The author calls this part exploratory.

**The chain "document, consistency, result" has not been tested.** `[inference]` In small firms the first link has not been studied at all: the narrow queries of the second pass yielded no peer-reviewed study, and a text search in reviews of 38 and 63 studies ([Yönel et al. 2026](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/70e2e70f-2212-40ae-b55f-cc1780d4d2ef/content), [Fluhrer, Brahm 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11846-025-00931-9)) found no analysis of brand books. The only direct mention in an academic study of startups turned out to be advice to create a corporate design book, with no comparison of firms with and without the document (`[expert; text]` [Bresciani, Eppler 2010](https://sonar.ch/documents/318205/files/bresciani_JPBM_2010.pdf)). The second link is correlational and contradictory: `[study; description]` among 285 German small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), consistency builds trust, which supports growth ([Eggers et al. 2013](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/worbus/v48y2013i3p340-348.html)), while `[study; text]` among 181 firms in North Macedonia there is no direct effect on financial results ([JEEMS 2017](https://www.imrpress.com/journal/JEEMS/22/1/10.5771/0949-6181-2017-1-83/pdf)). `[inference]` One cannot write "research shows that a brand book benefits small companies." The justification rests on the logic of reducing irreversible mistakes and coordinating people.

**Internal branding: channels between people matter, but the size of the effect is unknown.** `[study; abstract]` A meta-analysis of 38 studies names communication the main predictor of brand commitment, leadership the strongest measure for behavior, and brand understanding the most influential factor of behavior; HR measures give a moderate effect ([Afshardoost et al. 2023](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/internal-brand-management-brand-understanding-employee-brand-comm/)). Two more meta-analyses ([Prashar, Maity 2024](https://www.econbiz.de/Record/building-employee-commitment-through-internal-branding-a-meta-analytic-study-prashar-atul/10015047511), [Chhibber et al. 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/manrev/v75y2025i2d10.1007_s11301-024-00408-1.html)) are available only as abstracts; **we have not seen pooled coefficients, so the effect cannot be called strong or weak**. A survey of 167 top managers shows that informal management and empowerment are linked to behavior more strongly than formal instruments ([Henkel et al. 2007](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/10610420710779609/full/html)); this is correlation and self-assessment. `[study; description]` The only causal element in this section: a field experiment in which transformational brand leadership was successfully trained; the size and conditions were not checked, and it is not about documents ([Morhart et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkg.73.5.122)). `[inference]` The lines of research converge on channels between people being linked to results more strongly than formal means. But on the visual identity data the gap is small and depends on the model: by pairwise correlation, tools and support are not behind socialization; in regression they get .22 to .24 against .30 to .33; and socialization includes criticism for non-compliance. The conclusion "the document does not matter" does not follow, because no one has compared a good and a bad brand book under equal leadership. The measured outcome there is also different: customer service, not correct application of the style.

**Adjacent data on documentation run against the popular version.** `[transfer; text]` Two surveys at IBM (69 and 254 responses): respondents ranked five content problems above any presentation problem; 51.9% named ambiguity the top priority, although 26.5% encountered it often ([Uddin, Robillard 2015](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~martin/papers/ieeesw2015.pdf)). A small study of engineers: 68% agree that documentation lags behind, but 81% consider it useful and out of date; 6% reported spending significant time reading it, and in observation documentation was opened in 3% of events ([Lethbridge et al. 2003](https://courses.cs.duke.edu//cps196.1/fall11/classwork/Lethbridge-Singer-Forward-2003.pdf)). A methodological lesson transfers from this: surveys about brand book use probably overstate it. The uncertainty of the transfer is large: code has a checkable truth, and a brand does not.

**Knowledge transfer.** `[transfer; text]` In a questionnaire on 122 transfers of practices in eight companies, the biggest obstacles were the recipient's lack of capacity to apply the knowledge, not understanding the reasons, and difficult relationships, while motivation weighed much less; the survey is retrospective and correlational ([Szulanski 1996](https://josephmahoney.web.illinois.edu/BADM%20545_Spring%202008/Paper/Szulanski%20(1996).pdf)). `[transfer; abstract]` An eight-year natural quasi-experiment at Rank Xerox shows a gain from a working sample; according to the author's commentary, a wave without such a sample failed with equal support from management, and the advice is to copy closely and adapt later ([Jensen, Szulanski 2007](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v53y2007i11p1716-1730.html), [INSEAD](https://knowledge.insead.edu/strategy/knowledge-transfer-use-templates)). A template here means a working example, not a written specification, and it is a single case. `[inference]` No application of these theories to brand books was found. As an explanatory frame they are useful: failure is more often linked to people not understanding why and not knowing how to apply something than to unwillingness. That is why the goal of "100% compliance" is wrong; the goal is to distinguish acceptable from unacceptable variation.

**What about the boring-PDF version.** In a survey by a company that benefits from the opposite conclusion, `[vendor; text]` PDF is used by 72% of those who rate their guidelines as effective and 44% of those who rate them poorly; oral tradition gives 27% versus 55%, a web page 42% versus 21%, and among the "poor" only 9% consider the guidelines easy to find ([Demand Metric 2016](https://d2slcw3kip6qmk.cloudfront.net/marketing/press/webinar/The-Impact-of-Brand-Consistency-Report-New_Cover.pdf)). The figure caption confirms only that PDF leads among the effective; which series belongs to which group, the researcher reconstructed from the order of the data in the file. `[inference]` PDF here goes along with a formal program, so this is not proof that PDF helps. But the idea that a boring format explains neglect gets no support. Form does matter where it helps complete a task: a PDF is hard to scan and spreads as diverging copies (section 7), and findability works more strongly than design.

**Strictness and workarounds.** `[vendor; text]` At Demand Metric, non-compliant materials are released rarely or never by 24% with formal rules and no enforcement, 32% with informal rules, 38% with selective enforcement, and 58% with consistent enforcement; the "no enforcement" cell is small, and there are no significance tests. This does not support the idea that strictness breeds neglect, and it diverges from Henkel: the outcomes there are different (employee behavior and material compliance), and both are self-reported. `[study; text]` Beverland and coauthors, from interviews with designers and brand managers, describe how relying too much on existing frameworks hinders innovation, with the caveat that concern for consistency is not wrong in itself ([Beverland et al. 2015](https://purehost.bath.ac.uk/ws/files/120696551/JAMSdesignfinal.pdf)). `[study; text]` Ten agency strategists all value consistency and unanimously ask to loosen rigid guidance while keeping the key elements constant; this is the opinion of people who sell such systems, and there are no outcome data ([Gregersen, Johansen 2018](https://pure.au.dk/ws/files/172003139/Gregersen_2018_Corporate_visual_identity.pdf)). `[transfer; text]` The only external measurement nearby is about hygiene: in Los Angeles inspections, franchised outlets scored about a point below company-owned ones until the grade became visible to customers ([Jin, Leslie 2009](https://econ.umd.edu/sites/www.econ.umd.edu/files/pubs/ReputationAEJFinalVersion.pdf)). `[inference]` Two hypotheses to test: workarounds are caused by the cost of compliance at the moment of work, and compliance grows when the result is visible and the violator pays for it.

**The fate of historical manuals.** `[expert; description]` The NASA manual did not survive a change of administrator: in 1992 Goldin brought back the old mark, citing morale ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_insignia); `[document]` [NASA 2020](https://www.nasa.gov/image-feature/the-worm-is-back/)). The New York subway system, according to secondary descriptions, was widely implemented only from 1989: a crisis, the budget, and friction with the sign shops got in the way ([Urban Omnibus](https://urbanomnibus.net/2011/05/book-review-helvetica-and-the-new-york-city-subway-system/), [Eye](https://www.eyemagazine.com/review/article/the-font-now-arriving-at-platform-1)). `[document]` The T—Zh editorial policy is marked as no longer updated ([document](https://docs.google.com/document/d/14XdGIjVJLM_FsjHzyh5ca8PkffngykzXd2bLPHzA2ME/pub)). `[inference]` A document does not replace a budget, someone to carry it out, and the organization's stability. The reputation of "cult" manuals is, moreover, amplified by reissues sold by an interested party (`[vendor]` [Eye on Design](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/standards-manual-imprint-teams-up-with-aiga-to-reissue-1977-epa-graphic-standards-system/)). Neglected public copies occur at large companies too: at Starbucks, an expired certificate with an unchanged word count (`archive`); at Slack, a dead link to the Brand Center; at Mailchimp, © 2023; at Mastercard, the last requirements entry dated December 2023. These are not data on internal use.

Usage signals are rare and come from the companies themselves. `[document]` Monzo reports that in its first year it trained more than 700 employees in writing, made this part of onboarding, and embedded writers in teams ([Monzo blog](https://monzo.com/blog/2019/03/27/tone-of-voice-birthday)). No company shows a link between the document and results.

## 6. Brand voice: what is confirmed is narrow features and appropriateness, not adjectives

### Definitions are disputed, so you have to set your own explicitly

`[inference]` The formula "voice is constant, tone changes" remains a convention of product guides, not an agreed definition; no open source cites data in favor of this split. `[document]` [Mailchimp](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/) puts it this way, and [Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/design/content/voice-and-tone), [Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/content/voice-tone), and [NHS](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/content/voice-and-tone) are close to it. At [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/brand-voice-above-all-simple-human) (`[document; text]`) voice means "how we speak" and tone is part of it, while at [Intuit](https://contentdesign.intuit.com/ai/voice-tone/how-to-get-ai-to-sound-like-intuit/) voice means "what we say" and tone "how," that is, the exact opposite. [GOV.UK](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/right-tone/) and [Monzo](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice) do not separate the concepts. `[expert]` Moran at NN/g uses the single term tone of voice ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/)).

`[inference]` Our working definition: voice means the rules that always apply and can be checked on any text; tone means predefined shifts of these rules by situation. Its value is in checkability, not in being "correct." A skill that reads someone else's guide should not treat the words voice and tone as unambiguous.

### What experiments show and when informality hurts

We know almost all the studies from abstracts or descriptions; effect sizes are mostly not visible. The label for all rows is `[study]`.

Digest |

`[inference]` The most robust finding here: readers judge **the appropriateness of the voice relative to the relationship and the risk, not warmth as such**. The moderators repeat across studies: familiarity with the brand, involvement and risk, the relationship norm, service failure. Informality hurts an unfamiliar brand, at high stakes, and with a reader who expects a business exchange.

`[inference]` On service failure there is no real dispute: the studies measure different things, so one has to separate who is measured and which technique is tested. Friendly humor in response to a public complaint works on a par with an apology and compensation among observers; the complainant is not measured there (Béal, Grégoire). Emoji lower the competence rating; the abstract of Li and coauthors does not report a result for service failure, and the harm in this situation is claimed by secondary retellings. A humanlike bot lowers the satisfaction of angry customers if the problem is not solved (Crolic et al.), while a [2024 article](https://link.springer.com/10.1057/s41599-024-03212-0) (`digest`) shows a benefit from a warm bot style during a failure. Caution is sensible toward the affected customer; for public replies a direct ban does not follow from the data, and style does not replace a fix. There is no meta-analysis and there are no Russian data. The results most applicable to writing are narrow: who the subject is, and whether there is a concrete object instead of a general word. As a universal goal, "friendliness" is poorly supported.

The limits are serious. `[document]` An open tracker of marketing replications gives 5 successful attempts out of 45; the authors of these studies do not appear in the page's retelling; the tracker is not a random sample ([OpenMKT](https://openmkt.org/research/replications/)). No direct replications and no meta-analysis of the human voice were found; different studies define this voice itself by different features (`[study; abstract]` [Liebrecht et al. 2021](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbrese:v:132:y:2021:i:c:p:124-135)). Most experiments were done on online panels and scenarios in English. The summary "19% more satisfied" for Packard from a [secondary site](https://app.sciencesays.com/p/say-i-not-we-to-customers) must not be used: the primary source did not confirm it. `[inference]` A brand book can honestly promise consistency and lower risk, not sales growth.

### How public guides are built

11 guides were opened (`[document]`, Microsoft as text, the rest as digest): [Mailchimp](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/), [GOV.UK](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/), [Monzo](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice), [Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/design/content/voice-and-tone), [Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/foundations/content/voice-tone), [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/top-10-tips-style-voice), [Intuit](https://contentdesign.intuit.com/voice-tone/writing-for-intuit/), [PostHog](https://posthog.com/handbook/brand/tone), [Google](https://developers.google.com/style/tone), [NHS](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/content/voice-and-tone), [Kontur](https://guides.kontur.ru/principles/text/). The count below is approximate: one agent kept it, with no second annotator `[inference]`. Constancy of voice and variability of tone are claimed by about seven. A tone map by situation, channel, or role exists in about seven: Shopify goes through 8 situations with do and don't, Monzo sets the intensity of its principles by channel, Atlassian writes "more/less" lists based on what the user feels, Kontur distinguishes speakers. Before/after pairs appear in about six, inclusive rules in about five, numeric thresholds in only two: GOV.UK breaks up sentences longer than 25 words, and Kontur keeps to 12–15. A [2022 Monzo snapshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20221231231214/https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice/) (`archive`) still asks for sentences shorter than 20 words. The researcher saw automatic text checking only at PostHog: the Vale linter (a program for automatic text checking) in pull requests ([docs style guide](https://posthog.com/handbook/docs-and-wizard/docs-style-guide.md)). `[document; text]` Contrary to the first audit's tagging, PostHog has a full voice section with do and don't for headings, body text, errors, emails, and social media ([tone](https://posthog.com/handbook/brand/tone)).

The famous sample is not the best fit. `[document; text]` The Mailchimp voice page has four traits and no examples; "five" refers to the goals on a [neighboring page](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/writing-principles/). It used to be different (section 7), and we do not know why the map was dropped. Monzo today keeps three principles and a matrix of six channels: in support and operational emails there is no wit, in organic social media it is at its maximum ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice/)). `[inference]` For a small company, Microsoft Top 10 is more useful as a form, Kontur as a model of roles and numbers, Shopify as a map of situations, Monzo as a rule of application.

**Methods for deriving a voice are not validated.** `[expert]` Podmajersky offers a one-page voice chart: a column per product principle and six rows (concepts, vocabulary, verbosity, grammar, punctuation, capitalization); this is personal experience, the book was not read, and a [filled-in VTEX example](https://uxwriting.vtex.com/docs/principles/voice-chart/) is visible. `[study; digest]` NN/g's four axes were selected by the author from literary tone words without factor analysis, and a survey of 50 people tested two characteristics, but not the structure itself. `[study; text]` A corpus method (analysis of a large set of texts): companies chose successful texts, the authors extracted value words and pronouns and proposed implementing tone through word lists and frequency thresholds; there is no reader test ([Henriksen et al. 2004](https://www.cst.dk/vid/public/rapport-4-tone-of-voice.pdf)). `[inference]` The common set of inputs is: who the reader is and what they fear, which product decisions must be expressed, and which of its own texts the company considers successful. A workshop sets the form, not the input.

**Indistinguishability remains a hypothesis.** `[expert]` The thesis about interchangeable voices rests on opinions ([Lynch in Ragan](https://www.ragan.com/why-corporate-tone-of-voice-guidelines-fail/), [Efimov](https://habr.com/ru/articles/944950/)). Indirect signs: `[document]` Mailchimp and Intuit's AI section use the same words, plainspoken and genuine (Mailchimp belongs to Intuit), and almost everyone asks for clarity. No studies were found in which readers told brands apart by style alone. `[inference]` There are three questions here. Can a machine tell them apart: probably, by analogy with a classifier of online communities (`[transfer; digest]` [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13114v1.pdf)), but the model may be distinguishing topics, not voice. Can a reader tell them apart, and does it affect outcomes: there are no data, and the studies of brand language that we found do not answer this. The assumption that rules, roles, and bans distinguish more strongly than adjectives (Microsoft asks for contractions, GOV.UK limits them) remains a hypothesis. A simple test, itself not validated: remove the name and ask whether this voice would suit a direct competitor.

### The minimal section and our position on the disagreement

The notes disagree here: the review of small firms puts tone of voice on the list for "when the first non-founder writes on the company's behalf," while the voice audit proposes a minimal page of rules with 8–10 pairs of examples.

**Our position `[inference]`: a short block belongs to the mandatory core, and the full page is switched on by an event.** There are no direct data for either timing: the support for the first option concerns consistency, not voice; the support for the second is how often the techniques recur, and no one has tested whether such a set is sufficient. So the decision is made by logic. Some voice decisions are unavoidable already in the first text: in Russian, the choice of «ты» or «вы» runs through verbs and imperatives in messages, and the company makes it in any case, just implicitly. The event comes sooner than it seems: a freelancer, a support contractor, or a language model that writes to customers is exactly that "non-founder," and in a company that uses AI tools this can happen on the very first day. A short block costs little, because it is taken from texts already written. The argument against: while the founder personally reads all the texts, consistency rests on their presence, and an extra page may sit unused. That is why we leave the tone map, the glossary, and the checks for the event. The position will change if a comparison of companies with and without the block shows no difference.

| Address | Right away | «Вы» or «ты», letter case by channel (table below), name | `[transfer]` T/V experiments in neighboring languages, conditional on age, gender, and brand image; `[vendor]` Anketolog; `[document; text]` Gramota's Pismovnik (a letter-writing guide); `[document]` Kontur |

### Russian: many rules from English guides do not transfer

**No controlled Russian experiments on «ты» and «вы», brand register, and инфостиль (infostyle, a Russian school of plain, fact-first writing) turned up in nine targeted queries on the open web**; the eLibrary and CyberLeninka databases were not queried directly. `[transfer; text]` The closest study: a lab experiment on 274 students, in which simplified Bank of Russia press releases lowered inflation expectations only when the rate was raised ([Kartaev, Klachkova 2025](https://msuecon.elpub.ru/jour/article/download/1562/636)). This is a different sender and a different outcome. The rest here is linguistics and surveys. `[study; text]` Norms for «ты» and «вы» differ across groups and regions, and native speakers disagree even in simple cases ([Shmelev 2016](https://history.rsuh.ru/jour/article/viewFile/214/215)). `[vendor]` In an Anketolog survey of 1000 Russians, with no method stated, 82% want to be addressed with «вы», zoomers just as many, familiarity irritates 45%, and 85% prefer being addressed by a specific employee; these are stated preferences ([New Retail](https://new-retail.ru/novosti/retail/kakoy_stil_obshcheniya_s_brendami_predpochitayut_rossiyane_opros/)). `[expert]` Agency opinions in [DP.ru](https://www.dp.ru/a/2023/03/17/Perejdjom_na_ti) that «ты» suits young people diverge from these figures. `[transfer; text]` In a chatbot experiment on 284 residents of Switzerland, the winner depends on language, age, and gender; among German speakers "Sie" gives stable ratings, and the authors themselves call the influence small ([Ollier et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2021.691595)). `[transfer; digest]` Informal address works better for brands perceived as warm, formal address for those perceived as competent ([Leung et al. 2023](https://aminer.org/pub/632631c990e50fcafdf9fc2f/consumer-preference-for-formal-address-and-informal-address-from-warm-brands-and)). `[inference]` «Вы» is a sensible default: in neighboring languages it shows no general gain, but the German part of Ollier gives stable ratings, and Anketolog shows a stated preference. «Ты» should be chosen deliberately: it depends on age, gender, and brand image, and it exposes gender in the past tense. The lowercase letter on a website rests on the reference itself (table below), not only on Kontur's example.

The notes also disagree on what exists publicly at all. The voice audit calls Kontur the only detailed Russian guide, while the small-company audit reads ~9000 words of the [Редполитика Т—Ж](https://docs.google.com/document/d/14XdGIjVJLM_FsjHzyh5ca8PkffngykzXd2bLPHzA2ME/pub) [T—Zh Editorial Policy] with "no/yes" pairs. `[inference]` There are at least three guides: the living Kontur, the frozen T—Zh, and Ozon; the Ozon file itself was not opened, and the rules are visible from [two](https://habr.com/ru/articles/821383) [articles](https://habr.com/ru/companies/ozontech/articles/753022/) by the team (`digest`). Kontur's voice in its brand book is behind a login; Shift's tone is visible in a copy (`[document; archive]` [2026 snapshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20260212010230/https://shift.dodobrands.io/en/tov-dodo-pizza)). [Sber's guide](https://www.sberbank.com/promo/plain_language_guide/) is known only from a link; it could not be opened, and its content is unknown. At Alfa-Bank, T-Bank, and hh.ru only advice for other businesses was found. Address and gender are decided in writing almost only by Kontur, so a skill reading someone else's Russian guide infers the address from the texts.

**Инфостиль** `[expert; description]` is described by Ilyakhov and Sarycheva as simplicity and replacing judgments with facts, with eight groups of stop words ([Lifehacker](https://lifehacker.ru/pishi-sokraschai/)); the book was not read. Ozon checks texts with Glavred; no test of инфостиль on readers turned up in the queries we ran. `[inference]` This is basic clarity, not a voice: if everyone writes this way, clarity grows but distinctiveness does not. Stop words serve as an editor's heuristics, not as a hard ban.

The table below is `[inference]`. The case rule is checked against Gramota's direct text; the rest of the norm mostly through digests of Gramota and the Russian Grammar; these rules have not been tested in Russian.

| English rule | What happens in Russian | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Address the reader as you (GOV.UK, PostHog) | The choice sets distance and runs through the verb and imperative in messages; buttons in the infinitive remove it ([Ozon](https://habr.com/ru/articles/821383)) | A separate decision; check consistency in messages, not on buttons |
| English has no capitalization choice for «вы» | `[document; text]` [Письмовник Грамоты](https://gramota.ru/biblioteka/spravochniki/pismovnik/kak-pisat-vy-i-vash-s-propisnoy-ili-so-strochnoy-bukvy) [Gramota's Pismovnik]: to several persons or an indefinite group, including website visitors, lowercase. To one person in official documents and letters, capitalized, with the last word left to the author. Questionnaires, advertising, and leaflets as texts for repeated use: capitalized (Lopatin, Nechaeva, Cheltsova, per the Pismovnik) | Website and newsletter to an indefinite group: lowercase, straight from the reference. A personal or official letter to one person: capitalized, at the author's choice. Forms and leaflets: capitalized, which diverges from interface practice. Messages to one signed-in user are not mentioned: choose and write it down |

| Singular they (Microsoft, Mailchimp) | No equivalent. | "I" instead of "we" (Packard) | Person is visible from the verb without a pronoun; «я» in the past tense assigns the speaker a gender, «мы» ("we") does not | Count verb morphology, not words |

The answers reviewed say nothing about declining names in Latin script. Numerals were not checked against a reference | A template with a generic noun removes the problem; check the templates, not only the text |

`[document; text]` The Gramota journal writes that the capital letter is still supported by the rules, but the norm is clearly weakening in online correspondence; the share of half of all speakers cited there is one author's observation, not a measurement ([article](https://gramota.ru/journal/stati/pravila-i-normy/vy-s-propisnoy-bukvy-pravilo-ustarelo)).

Kontur's error messages are built like this: `[document]` the heading conveys the main point, then what happened and what to do, while generic confirmations are almost useless ([Kontur](https://guides.kontur.ru/principles/text/)). `[transfer; digest]` Controlled studies exist, but they come from HCI and are known only through abstracts: an apology improves the experience without canceling the effect of the system's own performance ([Tzeng 2004](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_57583991)), and it depends on mood and content ([Akgün et al. 2010](https://avesis.metu.edu.tr/yayin/d2ad3803-2df3-44a9-881c-7f2a13fff7ea/the-effect-of-apologetic-error-messages-and-mood-states-on-computer-users-self-appraisal-of-performance), 310 students); humor in a task without a failure increases liking without a loss in performance ([Morkes et al. 1999](https://hci.stanford.edu/seminar/abstracts/99-00/000225-morkes.html)). No experiments on user fault and instructions turned up in the queries. `[inference]` The error rules at Kontur, Ozon, and [Google](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/error-messages/set-tone) remain convention.

## 7. What a brand book should look like: a site with checkable rules, files alongside, data kept separate

### Medium: there are no comparisons, only mechanics and precedents

No studies comparing brand book media by use or consistency turned up in the queries we ran. `[transfer]` NN/g, based on observations of intranets and corporate sites, writes that a long PDF on screen disorients, scans poorly, and is harder to update, while an outdated version undermines trust; sample sizes are not given ([Nielsen, Kaley 2020](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/avoid-pdf-for-on-screen-reading/)). `[document]` GOV.UK, in an update of March 24, 2026, removed the PDF of its brand guidelines and kept the site, and the old address returns 410; no reason other than accessibility is given ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/govuk-brand-guidelines)). Selectel moved from an outdated agency PDF to a site, and rejected Figma because contractors and managers would have found it complicated ([Habr](https://habr.com/ru/companies/selectel/articles/867256/), `text`). At the same time, Help Scout keeps its guide in Figma ([brand](https://www.helpscout.com/brand/)), so a site is not the only answer. The table is `[inference]`.

| Medium | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Deep links, search, one current version, accessibility | Needs hosting and an owner; with client-side rendering, invisible to agents |
| PDF | A single snapshot, printing, handing over to a contractor, archiving | On-screen navigation, diverging copies, accessibility |
| Document or Notion | Cheap start, joint editing | Isolated samples, no comparisons: [Notion about itself](https://notion.notion.site/Notion-s-brand-usage-guidelines-How-to-use-Notion-s-brand-in-your-marketing-689045e1761348f887a3c5c80295e00f) (a weak case: the company writes in its own product), T—Zh in a Google Doc |
| Figma file | Components next to the designer's work | Closed to non-designers, limits on token import |
| Repository | History, review, automated checks, machine reading | A barrier for marketing and contractors |
| DAM or portal | Rights and files in one place | Closed, dependent on the vendor |

A defensible recommendation `[inference]`: a page for people in open HTML with permanent addresses, files in an archive, data in a repository or next to the site, PDF only as a dated snapshot. This is an opinion that rests on precedent, not a research result.

### Dated comparisons: there is no single path from PDF to a living site

### Length and structure: tasks on top, reference below

`[document]` In a separate sample on form, also of 19 documents and not the same as the large-company audit, the spread runs from one screen ([Linear](https://linear.app/brand)) to 44 substantive web pages ([GOV.UK](https://brand.design-system.service.gov.uk/sitemap), `text`). `[document; text]` Mastercard keeps about 88.5 thousand characters on a single [page](https://www.mastercard.com/brandcenter/us/en/brand-requirements/mastercard.html). The NYCTA binder has 174 sheets ([Sotheby's catalog](https://sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2014/the-new-york-sale-n09215/lot.36.html?locale=fr)), while the [reissue](https://standardsmanual.com/products/nycta-graphics-standards-manual-full-size-edition) has 356 pages; these are different editions. The word counts below were estimated by a tool. The smallest set read on live pages that meets all four brand book criteria: PostHog's foundations and visual identity, about 4000 words (1711 and 2336 in the Markdown versions, our direct count), at a company of about 200 people; Selectel is shorter by a rough counter, about 2500 words including navigation (`archive`). PostHog's first guide appeared at 30–38 people, but its content was not read ([commits](https://github.com/PostHog/posthog.com/commits/master/contents/handbook/company/brand-assets.md), [story](https://posthog.com/handbook/company/story)). For voice, [Sentry](https://develop.sentry.dev/frontend/sentry-voice/) (about 950 words) and [Monzo](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice/) (about 3000) are compact. At the other extreme, Help Scout itself gives the size as about 80 pages, with a team of five people and about a year of work ([article](https://articles.helpscout.com/blog/evolving-brand-design/)); the guide was not read.

`[inference]` Length depends on the number of channels and elements, not on the size of the company: GOV.UK has 44 pages because of the matrix of elements by channel. Assembling the minimum looks like this: 500–1000 words of meaning, about 1000 words of voice, a system sheet, files, and an owner. No such document exists in the sample; this is our assembly, and we do not claim a link "short document, strong brand."

The skeleton "foundation, elements, application, resources and contact" recurs. `[document]` Navigation more often goes by element, with tasks added as a second layer: Stanford's home page gives six cards such as "order business cards" and "generate a unit logo package" ([Stanford](https://identity.stanford.edu/)), while Supabase and Vercel start with a rule for choosing a file. `[inference]` A convenient architecture: task cards at the entrance, a tree by element as a reference, a cut by channel only where the values really differ, then an application page, resources, and contact. A one-pager works as an external or partner version; as a replacement for an internal guide it is not confirmed.

Research on reading and learning supports this form, but brand books have not been studied. A meta-analysis of case comparisons gives d = 0.60 across 57 experiments in the conference version (`text`, [Alfieri et al. 2011](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED528947.pdf)), while the 2013 article, judging by a fragment, gives 0.50. Contrasting cases prepare learners for an explanation and usually do not replace it (`text`, [Schwartz, Bransford 1998](https://www.wright.edu/sites/www.wright.edu/files/uploads/2017/Feb/event/Schwartz_Bransford_1998_TimeForTelling.pdf)). `[inference]` Hence the order for do/don't: a "right/wrong" pair, with an explicit rule and the reason next to it. There is no experiment on visual identity. Do/don't are disputed in practice too: `[document; text]` the Selectel author explains in a comment that they were dropped because a full list would bloat the document, a partial one would be a formality, and all layouts go through review anyway; readers ask for the opposite ([Habr comments](https://habr.com/ru/companies/selectel/articles/867256/comments)).

### What a specification page contains

Logo, color, and typeface as craft are covered in the neighboring document [visual-identity-craft.md](visual-identity-craft.md). Examples are `[document]`; Spotify and Supabase were checked as `text`. No one has tested what level of specificity is enough to reduce errors.

| Property | Checkable | Not checkable or flawed |
|---|---|---|
| Color | A token and HEX, RGB, HSL, OKLCH values ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/brand)); CMYK and Pantone for print ([GOV.UK print](https://brand.design-system.service.gov.uk/colour/print)) | Only an image ([Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/about/brand-assets/)); only names ([Linear](https://linear.app/brand), [Stripe](https://stripe.com/newsroom/brand-assets)) |
| Clear space | Half the height of the icon ([Spotify](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/design)); the height of a lowercase letter ([GitLab](https://design.gitlab.com/brand-logo/core-logo)) | "Generous space" ([Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/about/brand-assets/)); a variable with no definition, per the digest ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/brand-assets)) |
| Minimum size | In px and mm ([Spotify](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/design), [GitLab](https://design.gitlab.com/brand-logo/core-logo)) | No value ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/brand)) |
| Wrong application | Nine numbered cases with an image and a ban (`text`, [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/nasa_graphics_manual_nhb_1430-2_jan_1976.pdf)); bans with a condition for approval ([GOV.UK](https://brand.design-system.service.gov.uk/colour/govuk-blue)) | One ban and a reference to the contract ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/newsroom/brand-assets)) |

`[document; text]` NASA also explains the reason: red only on a white or light neutral background, because bright and dark neighbors weaken its effect. `[inference]` A minimal page template is this: a permanent address, what the element is for, values by channel, an annotated image, contextual rules, wrong examples with a reason, where to get the file, who decides on exceptions. The check is simple: can an outsider or a linter say "violated" without a designer? A value inside an image is available neither for copying, nor to an agent, nor to a screen reader. The specification should also mark what is fixed, what varies, and who approves an exception.

### Assets to deliver

`[inference]` The minimum for a small company, our synthesis and not a standard: a logo archive with named files (SVG and PNG, color and monochrome, light and dark background, the icon); a color table; a typeface with license, source, fallback, and size scale; the spelling of the name; 5–9 wrong examples with the reason; 2–3 templates for real tasks. It matters to name the license: `[document]` OFL 1.1 allows logos and websites, but a modified font may require renaming ([OFL](https://openfontlicense.org/)). `[inference]` A contractor can get the file but not the right. No section on font licensing turned up in what was read; this was not checked systematically. A ready-made component seems more reliable than prose for agents: the study by [Cha et al. 2026](https://pure.seoultech.ac.kr/en/publications/design-system-compliant-user-interface-generation-with-llm-agents/) and the experience of the company Polar, described without measurements (`[vendor]` [Polar blog](https://polar.sh/blog/orbit-llm-safe-design-system)), point the same way, but this is a direction, not proof. For people, the only support is the transfer from Jensen, Szulanski: in the van den Bosch survey, templates were used by 75% and considered the most important, but they are not linked to consistency on their own, and the author explains this by their wide availability.

### Accessibility: publish pairs, not a palette

`[document]` WCAG 2.2: text at least 4.5:1, large text 3:1 (from 18 pt, or from 14 pt bold), interface components and meaningful graphics 3:1; meaning must not be conveyed by color alone. Logo text is exempt, but the exemption does not extend to an interactive control or to low contrast as a design choice outside brand requirements. Thresholds are not rounded ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), [Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)).

`[inference]` An example of our calculation with the WCAG formula on published values: Supabase green #3ECF8E gives 2.00:1 on white and 8.54:1 on #1C1C1C; GOV.UK blue #1D70B8 gives 5.17:1 on white. This is not an accusation of a violation: the criterion depends on the color's role. Hence the rule: a brand book publishes not a palette but a table of allowed pairs with the number and the role, as text, not as an image. `[document]` GOV.UK shows the acceptable pairs as an image grid ([GOV.UK blue](https://brand.design-system.service.gov.uk/colour/govuk-blue)). That is enough for a person, but not for an agent.

### Versions and owner

`[document]` The best observed examples: a log with reasons and a team address at [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/govuk-brand-guidelines), a date and the source in a repository at [GitLab](https://design.gitlab.com/brand-logo/core-logo), a date and a link to the team at [NHS](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/design-system/styles/colour), contacts by request type at [Kontur](https://guides.kontur.ru/resources/logos/) and [Figma](https://www.figma.com/using-the-figma-brand/). The only document out of 24 with an explicit version, date, and next review date: Zapier's partner PDF, and as of the access date the link still holds the version whose review was scheduled for December 2025 ([PDF](https://partnerportal.zapier.com/sys/document/open/7Ot00000000003a00hE)). The oldest mechanism: NASA's index of current pages. `[document; text]` The scan with a "January 1976" cover contains a letter from Truly and a Stennis Space Center page (`[expert; description]` [Truly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_H._Truly), [Stennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stennis_Space_Center)), which means sheets were replaced while the document kept its identity.

The mechanism has shifted from replaceable pages to a release log, and design systems inherit engineering practice ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/community/whats-new/), [USWDS](https://designsystem.digital.gov/about/whats-new/)). The update label is misleading: `[document]` at Microsoft, the shared date on the pages probably reflects the site build, not a content edit ([Voice](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/brand-voice-above-all-simple-human)); `[document; text]` at Mozilla, seven sections are stamped with the same minute ([Logos](https://brand.mozilla.com/document/231#/-/logos)), and at IBM the footer shows November 5, 2026, while the last log entry is from December 5, 2024 ([What's new](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/whats-new)). `[study; digest]` A startup participant in [Pöhlmann et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-04600-w) says that with agile work, guidelines change more often, and it is hard to tell external users about it. `[inference]` The minimum: a date on the page, a single log with the reason and the action for the user, permanent addresses, an explicit status instead of a silently broken path, a contact by request type, and the owner as a role. No evidence was found that a date or a log reduces the use of outdated files.

A page that renders only on the client is invisible to an agent without a browser: `[document; text]` for [IBM Design Language](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/) the first-pass tool returned 38 characters, while in a browser everything is open without signing in; [brand.uber.com](https://brand.uber.com/) redirects to the Frontify platform, where the app crashed on first load.

The split is `[inference]`; the scheme of generating both from one file has not been publicly tested on a brand book and remains a hypothesis.

## 8. Decision table for a small company

**The whole table remains our inference.** No section has causal support, so the "Basis" column shows what a decision rests on, not proof. Criteria: irreversibility of the decision, the mechanism of knowledge transfer, and recurrence in documents; frequency in public documents is not considered a basis on its own. **There are deliberately no headcount thresholds here.** No tested threshold was found, neither by headcount nor by the first marketer; the range of 30–150 employees in Bresciani and Eppler describes the sample, and the review by Yönel and coauthors explicitly leaves the transition to formal brand management to future research. The answer to the question "from how many people" is this: there is no research; here are the working events.

The review of small firms puts purpose, vision, and values under "right away," while the notes on brand theory treat purpose, personality, and archetype as optional modules. We do not require a manifesto, because purpose has the weakest support (section 2), but we ask for the founder's decisions to be written down: in the small-firm literature, values serve as the source of the brand, and in the Swiss startups they turned out to be a gap. Company history also sits under "right away," but for a company without investors and with close customers, the product and relationships matter more (`[study; abstract]` [Petkova et al. 2008](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/crr.2008.27)). So we write a paragraph right away and postpone the section.

## 9. What this means for AI agent skills

The conclusions here are ours `[inference]`; the data used are labeled separately. The section is short on purpose: the goal of this stage is to understand the subject.

The skill starts with the question "who will read the document and what must they create," not with a list of sections, and it asks about events, not headcount. It sets the meanings of the words "identity," "voice," and "tone" explicitly. Its first job is to draw out what is implicit from the founder, because the founder may not see the link between themselves and the brand, and to check this against what the team does and what customers hear. A template is not derived from the frequencies in public documents, but individual techniques can be borrowed: "what we are not" from PostHog, channels from Monzo, mode choice from Sentry, "no/yes" pairs from T—Zh and Kontur.

The skill does not promise growth and does not quote the figures from section 5. The result should contain rules that can be checked and working samples, as well as an owner and a way to get a quick answer. The best data point to conditions that the document does not create by itself (leaders' example, up-to-dateness, knowing that a rule exists), so a skill that produces only a file solves just part of the task.

For voice, a layered scheme is sensible: first checkable constraints, then examples, then a separate deterministic checker. The grounds are indirect and English-language; there are no data for Russian. `[study]` On verifiable instructions, GPT-4 in 2023 showed about 77% strict prompt-level accuracy (`text`, [IFEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07911)). From examples, models approximate an author worse than people do, going from 2 to 10 examples gives almost nothing, selecting examples by topic similarity lowered attribution, and the texts drift toward an average tone (`digest`, [Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14543v1)). Hence: pick examples by type of situation, not by topic; a playful voice needs a stricter check and a human; we calibrate the judge model on examples labeled by people. No comparison of "adjectives, rules, examples" on a brand task was found, so no numeric expectations for the skill can be given.

The file for the agent holds only non-standard rules and does not drag in the company story, the important things come first, and a generated file is tested on tasks, because the mere fact that it was created does not count as success. The skill distinguishes the statuses of the tools from section 7 and does not call DTCG a W3C standard.

## 10. Limitations

**Search.** In the first pass, the web search limit shared by all researchers (200 queries per turn) ran out in the middle of the work. In the second pass each track had its own limit, and it did not run out: 2 queries out of 15 for browser reading, 22 out of 50 on brand standards, 38 out of 50 on schools, 39 out of 50 on voice. The work was stopped by a different limit, a guideline of about 80 tool calls: three tracks exceeded it, and some tasks were not done. Queries on the earlier gaps were run: Aaker and Kapferer, personality meta-analyses, small-firm brand books, trademarks, Russian experiments, error messages, franchises. A query run does not mean a study read: what remained closed is stated below. Queries about Lufthansa and Russian public joint-stock companies were not run; eLibrary and CyberLeninka as databases, Google Scholar, design management journals, and French- and German-language publications were not checked. So "not found" everywhere means "not found in the queries we ran," not "does not exist."

**Archive.** In the first pass web.archive.org did not open. In the second, the archive was read in a browser, and there are comparisons for eight documents (section 7). The snapshot date remains an upper bound; there is no full list of snapshots.

**What the second pass opened.** In a browser: Mozilla, IBM Design Language, part of NHS, Mastercard, and kontur.ru/brandbook, which had returned 403 to the first pass, Zapier, Notion, and the public parts of Netflix and Uber. In the text layer: the van den Bosch 2005 dissertation (chapters 4–7 and 9), parts of Wang, Heinberg, Eisend 2025 and of Crolic et al. 2022, Gregersen and Johansen 2018, Jin and Leslie 2009, Kartaev and Klachkova 2025.

**Three sites only in the archive.** Shift, brand.selectel.ru, and doublearrow.co.uk failed live and were read only as Internet Archive copies. The failure is technical and was observed from our environment: the first two time out on connection, the third presents a self-signed TLS certificate. Geographic blocking cannot be ruled out; there are no signs of a deliberate refusal to readers. Everything taken from these copies is labeled `archive`, and no section-level conclusion rests on them alone: next to Selectel and Shift stand the live Mozilla, NHS, and Kontur, and next to British Rail the NASA manual that was read. Creative.starbucks.com, with its expired certificate, was also read only in the archive.

**Digests.** Most first-pass web pages were read through a helper model's retelling, without images. The second pass checked eight such claims in a browser: the numbers mostly matched, and the errors were in counting homogeneous items and in tagging neighboring pages. The Mailchimp voice has four traits, PostHog has a full voice section, and the GOV.UK brand site has 44 pages and an accessibility statement. The Uber 2018 PDF is supported indirectly: the 2020 snapshot shows a guide with section dates from late 2018 and early 2019; the file itself was not checked. The digest erred in the second pass too: it attributed the norm «вы вошла» (formal "you" with a feminine singular past-tense verb, "you signed in") and claimed that the Pismovnik does not mention websites.

**Closed access and technical issues.** From outside, different causes give the same empty result, so closed access is confirmed only where there is a sign-in form or an access notice. Login: Netflix (one logo page is public), brand.mailchimp.com, ibm.com/brand, part of Kontur's brand book, Mastercard's card files. Client-side rendering with open content: IBM Design Language, Mozilla; the first pass wrongly put Netflix and Mailchimp here. Redirect and app crash: brand.uber.com. Bot protection, which we did not bypass: NHS; its color, typeface, logo, and downloads pages were not read. Certificate error: creative.starbucks.com, tochka.com/brand, doublearrow. Dead address: brand.slackhq.com. Connection refused: Telekom, Shift, Selectel. The browser did not open the Vignelli Center, although the address answers 200 to an automated client; the content was not read. 403 in the browser too: sentry.io/branding. wise.design shows captions without links. The Litres and Rosseti PDFs exceed the 10 MB limit.

**What was not read: books above all.** Not a single book was read. Tables of contents, publisher samples, record cards, or chapter lists: Wheeler and Meyerson, Romaniuk, Gerstner, Unit Editions Manuals 1, Fenton and Kiefer Lee, Handley, Podmajersky, Simmons, Yifrah, Ilyakhov and Sarycheva, Domnin. The authors' own texts instead of books: Dunford, Aaker's posts; a review with quotes instead of the book: Sinek. Only others' descriptions: Binet and Field, Rosenzweig, Collins and Porras, Stengel, Mark and Pearson, Ries and Trout, Olins, Kapferer, Sharp, Shaw, Carroll. Richards, Halvorson, and Rach are not confirmed.

**What remained closed.** The composition of the core and periphery in Jordá-Albiñana 2009 is still unknown: OpenAlex, Unpaywall, and Semantic Scholar show closed access, and closely related studies by the same group did not open. Most journal articles were read at the abstract level: the Datta and Stahl coefficients are not visible, study 3 in Li was not read, and the texts of Aaker, Kapferer, Hatch and Schultz, Balmer and Greyser are closed. Not opened: the Figma guides of Ozon and Help Scout, Sber's guide, a study of Burrows's delta on five brands, and the study by Koryakina and coauthors on the brand book.

**Historical manuals.** Only NASA was read directly: the OCR text layer of the scan, all headings and the letter, about 20 pages in more detail; the illustrations were not viewed; some sheets date from no earlier than 1989. NYCTA is known from the page of a publisher whose address has changed, from catalogs, and from reviews; EPA from the publisher and the 2017 press; British Rail from a description and a sheet list on a collector's site in an archived copy, with the scans not viewed; Lufthansa only from Wikipedia. We have not seen the sheets of these four manuals themselves.

**The Russian-language sample is thin.** Read: Kontur, the T—Zh Editorial Policy, Dodo's book, the Litres page without the PDF, Yandex's logo rules, Selectel and Ozon articles on Habr. Selectel and Shift were read from archived snapshots. Kontur is open, but the brand platform, segments, and voice require signing in. Tochka is unavailable. The Russian PDFs of VDNKh, SurGU, and GASU were not opened; aviasales.ru/brand returned 404. A fully open brand book of a large Russian organization at a live address could not be confirmed. Russian experiments were searched for in a targeted way on the open web, not in databases; access to journals was complicated by refused connections. No articles that study brand book composition empirically turned up in the queries we ran, and a Russian GOST based on ISO 20671 was not found in three queries.

**Pointers without content.** These studies exist but do not take part in the conclusions, and none of them studies written brand standards: Erdem and Swait 1998, Burmann et al. 2009, Park et al. 1986, Miles and Mangold 2004, Rettig 1991, Keller, Brand Report Card, King and Grace 2010, Vallaster and de Chernatony 2006. Kelleher 2009 was not checked. Candidates from memory, not confirmed: Sine et al. 2006, DeSantola and Gulati 2017, Larson and Czerwinski 1998. Minor discrepancies remain unresolved: the sample size in Belenzon et al., 90 or 96 articles in Sepulcri et al., d = 0.60 or 0.50 in Alfieri et al.

**Sample biases.** Survival: we looked at well-known companies, not at those that closed or those without a document. Publication: a page for journalists appears for different reasons than an internal guide; for 18 of the 21 brand centers of Spanish Merco 2018 companies, access is restricted to people connected with the company. Mixed stages: the documents are current, the companies have grown, and except in the case of PostHog it is impossible to say what existed when they were small. Missing outcomes: no document reports whether it affected recognition or the quality of materials. A noticeable share of the quantitative data on brand orientation comes from a small group of Finnish authors. The sections were counted without a second annotator in both passes.

**What was checked after assembly.** A script confirmed that all URLs and numbers in the report are present in the notes of both passes, except our direct counts for PostHog and the links below; this is a match check, not a check that the notes themselves are correct. The van den Bosch coefficients were checked only against the notes: the researcher read the primary source, and it was not reopened. The check of the first pass removed an overstated claim about the harm of informality in service failure (section 6), so similar errors elsewhere are not ruled out. Checked directly against the primary source on November 6: the GOV.UK changelog, the status of the DTCG report, the Style Dictionary release, the version and date of the DESIGN.md repository, the length of the brand-guidelines file, the size of the PostHog pages, and the contrast calculation; during the second-pass assembly, the Pismovnik and the Gramota journal article were also read, the responses of the three sites above were checked, and the abstract of Datta et al. 2017 was verified by search. Nothing else was rechecked against the primary source.

**Links.** All 304 addresses were checked automatically on November 6: 259 responded 200 or 206, and 45 gave a different response. There is one real 404: the Templafy PDF. Another 29 addresses returned 403, mostly Emerald and publisher DOI links closed to an automated client; Mastercard, meanwhile, opened in a browser. The remaining 15 failed because of rate limiting (NASA, Standards Manual), a server failure, or a connection error (Sostav, Netology, eprints.kingston.ac.uk, businesswire, and others). Among them are Gramota pages that opened in a browser and were read, so such a response does not mean the address is dead.
